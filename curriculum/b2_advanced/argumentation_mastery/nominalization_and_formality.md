### Introduction

In French administrative, journalistic, and academic communication, **nominalization (*la nominalisation*)** is the primary stylistic engine for elevating register from everyday conversational speech to formal, authoritative prose.

Nominalization is the grammatical process of transforming verbs, adjectives, or subordinate clauses into **noun phrases (*groupes nominaux*)**:
* Conversational verbal style:  
  * *Comme le coût des logements augmente rapidement, les jeunes ont de la difficulté à s'installer.*
* Elevated nominal style:  
  * **La hausse rapide du coût des logements entrave l'installation des jeunes ménages.**  
    /la oz ʁa.pid dy ku de lɔʒ.mɑ̃ ɑ̃.tʁav l‿ɛ̃s.ta.la.sjɔ̃ de ʒœn me.naʒ/

On the **TCF Canada** and **TEF Canada** examinations, nominalization is a mandatory skill for reaching **CEFR Level B2 (NCLC 7+)**:
1. In **Writing Task 3 (*Expression écrite — Tâche 3*)**, the strict word limit (120 to 180 words) forbids sprawling, repetitive sentences. Nominalization allows you to condense complex arguments into information-dense, elegant summaries.
2. In **Speaking Task 3 (*Expression orale — Tâche 3*)**, introducing an argument with a nominalized concept (*« Le vieillissement de la population soulève des défis majeurs... »*) immediately signals academic maturity to the examiner.
3. In Canadian professional workplaces, drafting executive briefs, memoranda (*notes de service*), and regulatory reports requires consistent nominal syntax.

This lesson explores the morphological rules of nominal derivation, maps out common suffixation paradigms, trains you in syntactic transpositions, and demonstrates how to calibrate nominal density without producing opaque jargon.

### Morphological Patterns of Verbal Nominalization

French verbs transform into action nouns through several productive morphological mechanisms: suffixation, zero-derivation, and irregular historical roots.

```
                                ┌─── Suffix -ation / -ition : créer -> la création, interdire -> l'interdiction
                                │
                                ├─── Suffix -ment : aménager -> l'aménagement, licencier -> le licenciement
                                │
PATTERNS OF NOMINAL DERIVATION ─┼─── Suffix -age / -ure : bloquer -> le blocage, fermer -> la fermeture
                                │
                                ├─── Suffix -ance / -ence : persister -> la persistance, émerger -> l'émergence
                                │
                                └─── Zero-Derivation (Radical) : réformer -> la réforme, baisser -> la baisse
```

#### 1. Suffixes -ation, -ition, and -tion (Feminine Nouns)
This is the most common and productive category for regular and irregular verbs:

| Verb | Action Noun | IPA Transcription | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **créer** | **la création** | /la kʁe.a.sjɔ̃/ | creation / establishment |
| **adopter** | **l'adoption** | /l‿a.dɔp.sjɔ̃/ | adoption / passing of a law |
| **déclarer** | **la déclaration** | /la de.kla.ʁa.sjɔ̃/ | declaration / statement |
| **diminuer** | **la diminution** | /la di.mi.ny.sjɔ̃/ | decrease / reduction |
| **disparaître** | **la disparition** | /la dis.pa.ʁi.sjɔ̃/ | disappearance |
| **interdire** | **l'interdiction** | /l‿ɛ̃.tɛʁ.dik.sjɔ̃/ | prohibition / ban |
| **dénoncer** | **la dénonciation** | /la de.nɔ̃.sja.sjɔ̃/ | denunciation / reporting |

#### 2. Suffix -ment (Masculine Nouns)
Typically applied to *-er* verbs and verbs denoting psychological or physical change:

| Verb | Action Noun | IPA Transcription | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **aménager** | **l'aménagement** | /l‿a.me.naʒ.mɑ̃/ | planning / development |
| **développer** | **le développement** | /lə de.vlɔp.mɑ̃/ | development / growth |
| **ralentir** | **le ralentissement** | /lə ʁa.lɑ̃.tis.mɑ̃/ | slowdown |
| **licencier** | **le licenciement** | /lə li.sɑ̃.si.mɑ̃/ | layoff / termination |
| **renforcer** | **le renforcement** | /lə ʁɑ̃.fɔʁs.mɑ̃/ | strengthening / reinforcement |
| **soulager** | **le soulagement** | /lə su.laʒ.mɑ̃/ | relief |

#### 3. Suffixes -age, -ure, and -ance/-ence
* **-age** (Masculine, often technical or mechanical processes):  
  * *bloquer* → **le blocage** (/lə blɔ.kaʒ/, *gridlock / blockage*)  
  * *recycler* → **le recyclage** (/lə ʁe.si.klaʒ/, *recycling*)  
  * *déménager* → **le déménagement** (/lə de.me.naʒ.mɑ̃/)
* **-ure** (Feminine):  
  * *fermer* → **la fermeture** (/la fɛʁ.mə.tyʁ/, *closure*)  
  * *rompre* → **la rupture** (/la ʁyp.tyʁ/, *rupture / breakdown*)
* **-ance / -ence** (Feminine):  
  * *tolérer* → **la tolérance** (/la tɔ.le.ʁɑ̃s/, *tolerance*)  
  * *exiger* → **l'exigence** (/l‿ɛg.zi.ʒɑ̃s/, *requirement / demand*)  
  * *résister* → **la résistance** (/la ʁe.zis.tɑ̃s/, *resistance*)

#### 4. Zero-Derivation: The Verbal Stem as a Noun
In many high-frequency verbs, the noun is formed simply by extracting the verb root without adding a suffix:
* *réformer* → **la réforme** (/la ʁe.fɔʁm/, *reform*)
* *refuser* → **le refus** (/lə ʁə.fy/, *refusal*)
* *baisser* → **la baisse** (/la bɛs/, *drop / decrease*)
* *hausser* → **la hausse** (/la oz/, *rise / increase*)
* *chuter* → **la chute** (/la ʃyt/, *fall / plunge*)
* *coûter* → **le coût** (/lə ku/, *cost*)
* *accueillir* → **l'accueil** (/l‿a.kœj/, *welcome / reception*)

### Deadjectival Nominalization: From State to Concept

Transforming descriptive adjectives into abstract nouns is essential when analyzing societal qualities and ethical values:

| Base Adjective | Suffix Pattern | Resulting Abstract Noun | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **accessible** | **-ité** | **l'accessibilité** (f.) | *L'accessibilité universelle aux transports en commun est prioritaire.* |
| **précaire** | **-ité** | **la précarité** (f.) | *L'inflation plonge des milliers de ménages dans la précarité.* |
| **équitable** | **-ité** | **l'équité** (f.) | *La réforme fiscale vise à restaurer l'équité entre les citoyens.* |
| **efficace** | **-ité** | **l'efficacité** (f.) | *L'efficacité énergétique des bâtiments réduit les coûts de chauffage.* |
| **souple** | **-esse** | **la souplesse** (f.) | *Le modèle hybride offre une plus grande souplesse aux employés.* |
| **isolé** | **-ment** | **l'isolement** (m.) | *Le travail à distance peut accroître l'isolement social des jeunes.* |

### Syntactic Transposition: Re-engineering Sentences

Nominalization is not merely a vocabulary swap; it requires restructuring the entire syntactic architecture of the clause.

#### 1. Transposing Subject-Verb-Object into a Prepositional Complex
In a verbal clause, the subject performs an action on an object. In a nominal phrase:
* The **verb** becomes the **head noun** (*le nom noyau*).
* The **direct object** becomes a complement introduced by **de** (*d'*).
* The **subject (agent)** is introduced by **par** or **de**.

*Verbal (B1)*:
* *Le gouvernement provincial a augmenté les taxes scolaires.*
*Syntactic transformation*:
* *augmenter* → **la hausse / l'augmentation**
* *les taxes scolaires* → **des taxes scolaires**
* *le gouvernement provincial* → **par le gouvernement provincial**
*Nominal (B2)*:
* **L'augmentation des taxes scolaires par le gouvernement provincial a suscité de vives critiques.**  
  /l‿og.mɑ̃.ta.sjɔ̃ de taks skɔ.lɛʁ paʁ lə gu.vɛʁ.nə.mɑ̃ pʁɔ.vɛ̃.sjal a sys.si.te də viv kʁi.tsik/

#### 2. Transposing Subordinate Causal Clauses
Conversational French relies on subordinate clauses with *parce que* or *comme*. Formal French compresses them into prepositional nominal phrases:

*Verbal (B1)*:
* *Parce que la population vieillit rapidement, nous devons réformer le système de santé.*
*Nominal (B2)*:
* **En raison du vieillissement rapide de la population, une réforme du système de santé s'impose.**  
  /ɑ̃ ʁɛ.zɔ̃ dy vje.jis.mɑ̃ ʁa.pid də la pɔ.py.la.sjɔ̃, yn ʁe.fɔʁm dy sis.tɛm də sɑ̃.te s‿ɛ̃.poz/

*Verbal (B1)*:
* *Bien que les profits aient chuté au premier trimestre, l'entreprise embauche.*
*Nominal (B2)*:
* **Malgré la chute des profits au premier trimestre, l'entreprise poursuit ses embauches.**  
  /mal.gʁe la ʃyt de pʁɔ.fi o pʁə.mje tʁi.mɛstʁ, l‿ɑ̃.tʁə.pʁiz puʁ.sɥi se.z‿ɑ̃.boʃ/

### The Peril of Hyper-Nominalization

While nominalization is an indispensable tool, excessive nominalization—known as **l'empilement nominal** (*nominal stacking*)—produces opaque, suffocating sentences that hinder readability.

#### The Warning Sign: Preposition Overload
If a single sentence contains four or five consecutive nouns linked by *de*, the style becomes bureaucratic and clumsy:
* Unreadable Stacking:  
  * *L'analyse de la mise en œuvre de la politique de gestion de la crise du logement par la mairie...* ❌
* Balanced Re-articulation:  
  * **La mairie analyse actuellement la mise en œuvre de sa politique du logement.** ✅

Aim for a dynamic balance: use nominalization to establish overarching concepts and transition points, paired with precise active verbs (*entraver, favoriser, susciter, engendrer, stimuler, freiner*).
