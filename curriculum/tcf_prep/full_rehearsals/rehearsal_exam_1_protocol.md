### Introduction

Welcome to the final module of **Path 04: TCF Canada Exam Prep** — **Full Rehearsals & Test-Day Logistics**.

Up to this point in your preparation, you have mastered each component of the TCF Canada in relative isolation:
- In **Exam Mechanics**, you trained predictive listening sweeps, distractor elimination, and strict reading pacing.
- In **Writing Speed**, you developed unassisted keyboard muscle memory, precise word-count calibration, and rapid synthesis paraphrasing.
- In **Speaking Pressure**, you built modular self-introductions, 2-minute scrap paper shorthand, and 4.5-minute monologue stamina.

However, performing well in 20-minute isolated drills is fundamentally different from sitting through the **full, grueling 2-hour-and-47-minute endurance marathon of the official TCF Canada examination**.

When you enter an official test center, you do not face tasks one at a time with leisure to rest between them. You face a long battery: **35 minutes of intense listening**, **60 minutes of dense reading comprehension**, **60 minutes of high-speed typing**, and **12 minutes of live oral interview** with an examiner (the order of sections and any breaks depend on your centre; check your convocation). Under these conditions, the greatest threat to your score is not a lack of French vocabulary—it is **cognitive exhaustion and late-session decision fatigue**.

The **Rehearsal Exam 1 Protocol** establishes the exact physical, psychological, and technical conditions required to execute your first **Full Official Mock Sitting**. This simulation produces an uninflated, audit-grade diagnostic baseline across all four competencies, identifying your vulnerabilities before they can jeopardize your permanent residency application.

### The psychological imperative of the full-dress 3-hour rehearsal

Why is a full-dress rehearsal non-negotiable? In cognitive psychology, this is known as **state-dependent retrieval and stress inoculation**. If you only practice French in comfortable 30-minute intervals while sipping coffee, your brain learns to access grammar and vocabulary only in low-cortisol states.

On official exam day, your brain will experience:
1. **Adrenaline and Cortisol Spikes**: Elevated heart rate and tunnel vision during the opening minutes of Listening.
2. **Visual Eye Strain**: Working through 39 reading items, some of them long, dense texts, on a computer monitor for 60 continuous minutes.
3. **Typing-Induced Muscle Tension**: Composing three texts in 60 minutes without autocorrect or spellcheck.
4. **Social Evaluation Anxiety**: Speaking in a foreign language under the scrutiny of an official examiner, face to face.

#### The Cognitive Fatigue Curve

Many candidates see the same pattern in their own practice sittings:
- **Early Reading items** (the easier ones): accuracy holds up well.
- **Late Reading items** (the harder ones): accuracy drops more than the difficulty alone explains, because concentration fades after more than an hour of continuous French decoding.
- **Task 3 of Writing**: the text comes out shorter than planned because typing slows down in the final minutes of the test.

Completing a full-dress simulation trains your neural pathways to endure this metabolic depletion, proving that your B2 skills remain accessible even when you are physically and mentally fatigued.

During this rehearsal, you must treat your desk as a locked test center. You are **strictly forbidden** from:

- Taking an unauthorized snack or bathroom break during active test sections.

Any violation of these rules invalidates your diagnostic data and produces an artificially inflated score.

### The four-stage rehearsal protocol and interface configuration

To stay as close as possible to official test conditions, execute your mock sitting according to this master protocol:

```
[00:00 - 00:10] Stage 0: Workstation Calibration & Hardware Verification
[00:10 - 00:45] Stage 1: Compréhension orale (35 min / 39 items / Audio played ONCE)
[00:45 - 01:45] Stage 2: Compréhension écrite (60 min / 39 items / 15-15-30 study pacing)
[01:45 - 01:55] Stage 3: The 10-Minute Tactical Intermission (Hydration, Reset)
[01:55 - 02:55] Stage 4: Expression écrite (60 min / 3 tasks / Zero autocorrect)
[02:55 - 03:07] Stage 5: Expression orale (12 min / 3 tasks / Continuous recording)
[03:07 - 03:45] Stage 6: Scoring Audit, Error Autopsy & NCLC Mapping
```

#### Hardware & Software Calibration (Stage 0)

Before launching the test:
1. **Operating System Layout**: Set your physical keyboard to the layout your test centre uses (ask them in advance: often a QWERTY layout such as Canadian Multilingual Standard in Canada, AZERTY in France). Verify that dead-key accents work cleanly in a plain text editor.
2. **Browser Lock-Down**: Disable all browser extensions (Grammarly, Google Translate, LanguageTool). Close all background applications, messaging apps, and email clients.
3. **Audio Gear**: Connect closed-back headphones. Adjust the volume during the sound-check screen so that dialogue is crisp and clear at ~65 dB.
4. **Scrap Paper Station**: Place two blank sheets of A4 paper and a sharpened pencil on your desk for Task 2 Speaking notes and Reading calculations.

#### The 10-Minute Tactical Intermission (Between Stages 2 and 4)

Break arrangements at official centres are not published and vary from one centre to another (check your convocation), so do not count on a specific pause on test day. In your rehearsal, schedule a 10-minute break between the receptive and productive sections and practise using it well.

Do not waste this interval looking at your phone. Execute the **Somatic Reset Routine**:
- **Physical Movement**: Stand up, stretch your neck and shoulders, and walk for 3 minutes to restore cerebral blood flow.
- **Hydration**: Drink 200 ml of water. Avoid sugary sodas or energy drinks, which cause rapid insulin spikes followed by cognitive crashes.
- **Somatic Breathing**: Practice 4-7-8 tactical breathing (inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 7 seconds, exhale slowly through the mouth for 8 seconds) to reset your autonomic nervous system from sympathetic fight-or-flight back to calm analytical focus.

### Post-mock diagnostic triage and NCLC baseline mapping

The moment your 12-minute oral recording concludes, your rehearsal moves into its most critical phase: **the Diagnostic Triage**. A test score is useless if it does not reveal the exact root cause of every lost point.

#### IRCC TCF Canada → NCLC Equivalency Table

Map your performance across all four competencies onto the official Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) equivalency table:

| NCLC | Compréhension orale (0–699) | Compréhension écrite (0–699) | Expression écrite / orale (/20) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 10+ | 549–699 | 549–699 | 16–20 |
| 9 | 523–548 | 524–548 | 14–15 |
| 8 | 503–522 | 499–523 | 12–13 |
| **7 (TARGET)** | **458–502** | **453–498** | **10–11** |
| 6 | 398–457 | 406–452 | 7–9 |
| 5 | 369–397 | 375–405 | 6 |
| 4 | 331–368 | 342–374 | 4–5 |

There is **no official conversion from a number of correct answers out of 39 to a score out of 699**: items are weighted by difficulty. Use practice material that reports a score on the 0–699 scale, and treat any raw count as a rough progress indicator only. Expression écrite and Expression orale each receive one overall score out of 20.

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--tip" markdown="1">

#### The "All-Four NCLC 7" Standard

To claim the **additional Express Entry CRS points for French** (50 points if your English is CLB 5+, 25 points if it is CLB 4 or lower), IRCC requires you to score **at least NCLC 7 in all four competencies on the same test report**. 
- If you score NCLC 9 in Listening, NCLC 8 in Reading, NCLC 8 in Speaking, but slip to **NCLC 6 in Writing**, you receive **zero CRS bonus points**. 
- Your weakest skill determines your immigration outcome. Full-dress rehearsals identify which single skill threatens your entire application.

</div>

#### The Diagnostic Error Autopsy Matrix

For every question missed in Listening or Reading, and for every deduction in Writing and Speaking, record a diagnostic entry in your score log categorized by error type:

1. **Linguistic Gap (Lexique / Grammaire)**: You simply did not know the meaning of an essential word (*vétusté, déboires, concéder*) or misidentified a subjunctive mood.
2. **Distractor Trap (Piège psychométrique)**: You understood the audio or text, but were lured by a literal lexical echo, a reversed polarity adverb (*ne... guère*), or a chronological misdirection.
3. **Pacing Panic (Gestion du temps)**: You ran out of time, rushed through items without reading choices, or lingered on an item instead of answering and moving on.
4. **Cognitive Fatigue (Effondrement d'endurance)**: Errors concentrated in the final 15 minutes of the section due to mental exhaustion.

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--tip" markdown="1">

#### Québec vs France

If you sit the TCF Canada in an accredited test center in Canada (such as Alliance Française in Toronto, Ottawa, Montreal, or Vancouver) rather than in Europe or North Africa, prepare for distinct hardware and test-center ergonomics:
- **Physical Keyboard Layout**: Keyboard layouts are not standardized across centres and are not published. Canadian centres often use QWERTY keyboards (for example, Canadian French or Canadian Multilingual Standard), while centres in France typically use AZERTY. **Ask your centre which layout it uses** and practise on that layout beforehand; switching layouts on test day will slow your typing considerably.
- **Test Center Acoustic Ambience**: In many centres, candidate workstations are separated by standard office dividers rather than isolated soundproof booths, so you may hear other candidates typing or moving around. Rehearsing in environments with mild ambient background noise builds vital cognitive immunity to real-world exam distractions.

</div>
