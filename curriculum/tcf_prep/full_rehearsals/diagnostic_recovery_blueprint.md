### Introduction

Completing your first full-dress TCF Canada mock examination (*Rehearsal Exam 1*) is often an emotional shock. Even candidates who demonstrated solid competence in isolated grammar drills and reading exercises often find their scores unexpectedly suppressed when all four components—Listening (35 min), Reading (60 min), Writing (60 min), and Speaking (12 min)—are tackled in a single continuous three-hour sitting.

A candidate might discover that their Compréhension orale score plummeted from a projected NCLC 8 to an NCLC 5 because a single missed announcement on Item 24 triggered panic that spilled over into the next four questions. Another candidate may realize that after spending 42 minutes perfecting the easier early texts of the Reading section, they were forced to guess blindly on the final eight, hardest items. In Writing, a candidate who wrote an articulate 190 words on Task 3 realizes they ignored the 120–180-word range and lifted whole phrases from the documents—both of which weigh on task completion.

A diagnostic score log is not a judgment; it is a **forensic blueprint**. In psychometrics, raw scores merely show *that* you failed an item; a diagnostic audit reveals *why* you failed it. 

This lesson provides the **Diagnostic Recovery Blueprint**: an analytical system to triage your Mock Sitting #1 score sheet, categorize errors into four distinct failure modes, and execute high-yield remedial micro-drills over a structured seven-day sprint before attempting Rehearsal Exam #2.

---

### Lesson overview

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Triage your Rehearsal Sitting #1 score log across the **Four Diagnostic Failure Modes** (Linguistic Gaps, Psychometric Distractor Traps, Pacing Deficits, and Cognitive Fatigue Collapse).
- Execute the **Targeted Point Reclamation Protocols** for each skill to reclaim lost points in Listening and Reading.
- Deploy the **Writing Length & Paraphrase Recovery Protocol** to stay within the word ranges and eliminate direct text lifting in Task 3.
- Repair oral hesitations and monologue freezes using the **Sustained Ideation Drill**.
- Implement an intensive **7-Day Corrective Sprint Schedule** to convert diagnostic weaknesses into automated strengths prior to Mock Sitting #2.

### The four diagnostic failure modes: Identifying the root cause

When analyzing missed items on your score sheet, resist the urge to say *« J'ai fait une bêtise »* (*I made a silly mistake*). In high-stakes testing, every error leaves a forensic trace. 

You must classify every lost point into one of four failure modes:

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| THE FOUR DIAGNOSTIC FAILURE MODES                                 |
|                                                                   |
| 1. LINGUISTIC VOID (Lexique / Syntaxe)                            |
|    - Root Cause: Unfamiliarity with an essential word or mood.    |
|    - Signature: You did not understand the text or audio script. |
|                                                                   |
| 2. DISTRACTOR TRAP (Piège Psychométrique)                         |
|    - Root Cause: Acoustic/lexical echoes, reversed polarities.    |
|    - Signature: You understood the text, but chose the wrong MCQ. |
|                                                                   |
| 3. PACING DEFICIT (Gestion Chronométrique)                        |
|    - Root Cause: Lingering on early items; running out of time.   |
|    - Signature: Blind guessing on the last 5-8 questions.         |
|                                                                   |
| 4. COGNITIVE FATIGUE COLLAPSE (Effondrement d'Endurance)          |
|    - Root Cause: Glucose depletion; sensory overload after 90 min.|
|    - Signature: Errors clustered in the final 15 minutes.         |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

#### Detailed Autopsy of the Four Modes

#### 1. The Linguistic Void (Lacune Lexicale ou Grammaticale)
- **Manifestation**: You read a B2 text discussing **« l'engouement suscité par les énergies renouvelables »** and could not determine whether *l'engouement* meant *distrust*, *enthusiasm*, or *indifference*.
- **Remediation**: Passive vocabulary flashcards will not suffice. You must build an **Anki Context Sentence Deck** containing the target word embedded in an authentic journalistic sentence with its phonetic transcription:
  **« L'engouement du public pour ce projet ne faiblit pas. »** — /l‿ɑ̃.ɡu.mɑ̃ dy py.blik puʁ sə pʁɔ.ʒɛ nə fɛ.bli pa/ (*The public's enthusiasm for this project is not weakening.*)

#### 2. The Distractor Trap (Le Piège Psychométrique)
- **Manifestation**: In Listening, the speaker stated: **« Nous n'avons guère de doutes quant à la réussite de ce programme »** — /nu n‿a.vɔ̃ ɡɛʁ də dut kɑ̃.t‿a la ʁe.y.sit də sə pʁɔ.ɡʁam/ (*We have hardly any doubts regarding the success of this program*). You heard the word *doutes* and selected the distractor stating that the speaker was skeptical.
- **Remediation**: The error was caused by misinterpreting the restrictive negation **ne... guère** (*hardly / scarcely*). You must drill the **Reversed Polarity Checklist** to neutralize deceptive negative adverbs (*à peine, nullement, ne… guère, ne… point*).

#### 3. The Pacing Deficit (Le Déficit Chronométrique)
- **Manifestation**: In Reading, your score sheet shows correct answers for Items 1 through 24, but Items 32 through 39 are marked wrong because you ran out of time and clicked random choices in the final 60 seconds.
- **Remediation**: Your pacing architecture on Tier 1 and Tier 2 was too slow. You allowed the easy early documents to consume 22 minutes instead of 15 minutes. You must drill **Speed Skimming Protocols** to lock in your personal Checkpoint 1 at 45:00 on the countdown clock.

#### 4. The Cognitive Fatigue Collapse (L'Effondrement d'Endurance)
- **Manifestation**: In Writing Task 3, your grammar, which was pristine in Task 1, suddenly deteriorated into elementary errors: missing agreement on past participles, dropped accents on *à* and *où*, and fragmented sentences.
- **Remediation**: Your working memory crashed due to glucose depletion. You must enforce the **10-Minute Somatic Intermission Protocol** (hydration, deep breathing, posture adjustments) and practice writing essays while physically and mentally tired.

### The targeted recovery prescriptions: Micro-drills for rapid point reclamation

Once you have diagnosed your failure modes, deploy these targeted remedial prescriptions during your practice sessions:

#### Prescription 1: The Listening Prediction Reset Drill (For Receptive Traps)
If you missed several items in the middle of the Listening section:
- Open a TV5Monde or RFI listening exercise.
- **Execute the Quick Eye Sweep**: Before playing each audio track, read the four options. Highlight the **semantic fault line** (the word or concept that distinguishes each option from the other three). In practice you control the pace; on test day, use whatever time you have before the recording starts.
- Predict whether the audio will feature formal administrative French or informal conversational reductions (*y a*, *ch'sais pas*).
- Listen once without pausing. Commit to a choice as soon as the audio ends.

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--warning" markdown="1">

#### Exam tip: The "Five-Second Drop" Audit

Did you allow a missed audio item to poison the subsequent question? Review your score log: if you missed Item 28, did you also miss Item 29?
- If errors occur in **clusters of two or three consecutive questions**, you are suffering from the **Domino Freeze Trap**.
- Force yourself to recite the drop mantra the moment an audio finishes: **« Question passée, point oublié, cap sur la suivante »** (*Question finished, point forgotten, focus on the next*). Each recording plays only once: pick your best guess immediately and move your attention to the next question.

</div>

#### Prescription 2: The Reading Checkpoint Discipline Drill (For Pacing Deficits)
If you left reading questions unanswered or rushed through Tier 3:
- Set a countdown timer for **30 minutes** and attempt **Items 1 to 27 only**.
- **Rule**: At minute 15:00, you must submit Item 15 regardless of certainty. At minute 30:00, you must submit Item 27.
- This drill (a personal pacing heuristic, not an official rule) conditions your internal clock to spend about 60 seconds on the short early notices and about 75 seconds on the mid-section texts, so that you bank 30 full minutes for the longer, harder texts at the end of the section.

#### Prescription 3: The Writing Task 3 Paraphrase Matrix (For Plagiarism & Length Deductions)
If your Task 3 leaned on copied phrases or fell outside the 120–180-word range:
- Take two opposing opinion texts (approx. 50 words each) from *Le Devoir* or *Radio-Canada*.
- Apply the **Three-Step Transformation Algorithm**:
  1. Convert all active verbs into formal nominalizations (*« Le télétravail augmente la productivité »* $\rightarrow$ **« L'essor du travail à domicile favorise l'efficacité des salariés »**).
  2. Invert polarity using antonyms (*« Cela détruit les petits commerces »* $\rightarrow$ **« Cette mesure fragilise considérablement le commerce de proximité »**).
  3. Frame the debate with sophisticated reporting verbs (**« faire valoir »**, **« déplorer »**, **« redouter que »**).
- Draft the synthesis of the two documents (about **40–60 words**) + your personal stance (about **80–120 words**), aiming for about **155 words** in total (e.g., 50 + 105). Verify your word count (a word is any string between two spaces: *j'ai* = 1 word) and check for zero verbatim copying.

#### Prescription 4: The Spoken Monologue Stamina Drill (For Task 3 Monologue Freezes)
If you stalled in Speaking Task 3 before the 4:15 mark:
- Pick an abstract prompt question.
- Draw a simple **Three-Pillar Grid** on scrap paper:
  - *Pilier 1 : Économique / Fonctionnel*
  - *Pilier 2 : Humain / Psychologique*
  - *Pilier 3 : Sociétal / Éthique*
- Set a timer for **4 minutes and 30 seconds**. Speak continuously.
- When you finish an argument, do not pause: immediately deploy a high-register thinking filler:
  **« Cela étant posé, il convient d'élargir l'analyse à une dimension plus humaine... »**  
  — /sə.la e.tɑ̃ po.ze il kɔ̃.vjɛ̃ d‿e.laʁ.ʒiʁ l‿a.na.liz a yn di.mɑ̃.sjɔ̃ ply.z‿y.mɛn/

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--tip" markdown="1">

#### Québec vs France

When reviewing your diagnostic listening errors, check whether mistakes occurred disproportionately on audio tracks featuring Canadian accents:
- **Phonetic Affrication**: In spontaneous Québec French, /t/ and /d/ followed by high front vowels (/i/, /y/) are affricated to [ts] and [dz] (e.g., *tu* sounds like *tsu*, *dimanche* sounds like *dzimanche*).
- **Vowel Diphthongization**: Long vowels like /ɛː/ in *fête* or *tête* are diphthongized into [faɛ̯t], [taɛ̯t].
- **Lexical False Friends in Prompts**: Notice items referring to **le dépanneur** **(QC)** (corner convenience store vs. repairman in France), **le char** **(QC)** (car vs. tank/chariot), **la facture** **(QC)** for a restaurant bill (heard alongside *l'addition*, which is also normal in Québec), or **le traversier** **(QC)** (ferry).
If your diagnostic autopsy indicates an auditory deficit on Canadian voices, replace generic European radio with daily Radio-Canada podcasts (*Midi info*, *Le 15-18*) during your 7-day corrective sprint.

</div>
