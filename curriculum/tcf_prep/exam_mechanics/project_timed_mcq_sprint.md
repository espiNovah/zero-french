### Introduction

Welcome to the capstone project of **Exam Mechanics & Distractor Dissection** in **Path 04: TCF Canada Exam Prep**.

In the previous four lessons of this module, you systematically deconstructed the psychometric architecture of the computer-delivered TCF Canada (*TCF-SO*):
1. **Listening Prediction and Choice Scanning**: You learned how to use your pre-listening scan, conduct vertical semantic sweeps, and prime lexical fields before the audio begins.
2. **Distractor Dissection**: You mastered recognizing restrictive negations (*ne... que*, *guère*), false friends (*éventuellement*, *ignorer*), and chronological misdirection designed by test psychometricians to lure careless candidates.
3. **Reading Pacing Blueprint**: You internalized the 15 / 15 / 30 personal time budget, moving briskly through the easier opening items and preserving 30 dedicated minutes for the harder multi-paragraph texts at the end.
4. **The Five-Second Drop Rule**: You acquired the emotional discipline and somatic protocols required to answer a missed audio question with your best guess and let it go immediately, halting catastrophic cognitive domino collapses.

Now, you must fuse these four mechanical skillsets under authentic, continuous test-center pressure.

The **Back-to-Back Timed MCQ Sprint** is a 95-minute endurance trial that combines the **35-minute Compréhension orale** (39 items) directly with the **60-minute Compréhension écrite** (39 items). Each recording plays only once, and you cannot pause the clock; break arrangements on test day are not published, so check your *convocation* and the FEI candidate rules, and train as if there were none. Your ability to sustain sharp analytical focus across 78 consecutive multiple-choice questions determines whether you achieve the required **NCLC 7 threshold** (minimum 458 points in Listening, 453 points in Reading) or fall victim to late-session cognitive fatigue.

### Before you start

To produce an uninflated, audit-grade baseline of your receptive exam performance, set up your testing environment as close to real exam conditions as you can:

- **Uninterrupted Testing Block**: Block out exactly **1 hour and 45 minutes** (10 minutes for setup, 35 minutes for listening, 60 minutes for reading). You must not pause the timer, check your phone, open dictionary tabs, or take a recess between sections.
- **Hardware & Peripherals**: Take this simulation on a desktop or laptop computer with a physical mouse or trackpad. Do not complete this on a smartphone screen; reading dense multi-column texts on a mobile screen distorts your visual scanning speed.
- **Audio Gear**: Use closed-back over-ear headphones or well-fitted in-ear monitors. In a test centre, you may hear ambient noise from adjacent keyboards and neighboring test-takers.
- **Testing Material Sources**: Prepare an unattempted 39-question listening session and an unattempted 39-question reading session from reliable practice sources:
  - [TV5Monde — Apprendre le français](https://apprendre.tv5monde.com/fr) (free practice material; not official TCF items).
  - [France Éducation international — Exemples d'épreuves TCF](https://www.france-education-international.fr/).
- **Diagnostic Logging**: Have your diagnostic score tracker ready (or open a clean markdown document `tcf-mcq-sprint-log.md`) to record question-by-question timestamps, distractor classifications, and emotional drop ratings.

#### Part 1: Compréhension orale Simulation (35 Minutes, 39 Questions)

Launch your 39-question listening test. From the moment the first question appears on screen, execute the **Listening Mechanics Protocol**:

Items get progressively harder; FEI publishes no item-number→level map, so the phases below are described by difficulty only.

1. **Phase 1: Early, easier items**:
   - Identify the communicative setting within the first two seconds (*gare, magasin, restaurant, bureau, message téléphonique*).
   - Beware of acoustic trap words: when the question asks about *le train pour Lyon*, do not click an option simply because you heard the word *Lyon* if the speaker said **« Le train en provenance de Lyon a dix minutes de retard »** — /lə tʁɛ̃ ɑ̃ pʁɔv.nɑ̃s də ljɔ̃ a di mi.nyt də ʁə.taʁ/ (*The train arriving from Lyon is ten minutes late*).
   - Answer as soon as the audio ends and lock your attention onto the next item immediately.

2. **Phase 2: Middle items**:
   - Apply the **Eye Sweep**: identify the grammatical subject and main verb of all four choices before the audio starts.
   - Watch for explicit negations and nuance markers: **« Ce n'est pas tout à fait ce qui était convenu »** — /sə nɛ pa tu.t‿a fɛ sə ki e.tɛ kɔ̃v.ny/ (*That is not quite what was agreed upon*).
   - If an answer is not obvious after listening, eliminate the two most blatant distractors, make an educated guess between the remaining two, and move forward.

3. **Phase 3: Later, hardest items**:
   - Deploy **Active Ideational Tracking**: do not transcribe words mentally; map the logical stance of the speakers (*adhésion, scepticisme, désapprobation polie, résignation*).
   - **Enforce the Five-Second Drop Rule**: If you lose the thread of a fast radio debate or an academic interview, the recording will not play again: click your best guess immediately and force your eyes to the next question. Never let a missed question on item 31 cost you item 32 as well.

#### Part 2: Compréhension écrite Simulation (60 Minutes, 39 Questions)

The moment the listening test ends, your 60-minute reading timer begins. Immediately activate the **15 / 15 / 30 Pacing Blueprint**:

The item ranges below are personal pacing checkpoints, not official levels.

1. **Tier 1: Items 1 to 14 (Easier Short Documents — Budget: 15 Minutes)**:
   - Typical documents: messages, brief emails, opening hours, flyers, classified ads.
   - Target pace: **about 1 minute per item**.
   - **Checkpoint 1 (Clock reads 45:00 remaining)**: You must have answered item 14. If your clock shows 43:00, you are behind schedule. Accelerate immediately by eliminating decorative options and clicking the direct factual match.

2. **Tier 2: Items 15 to 25 (Medium-Length Texts — Budget: 15 Minutes)**:
   - Typical documents: job descriptions, administrative notices, consumer advice articles, cultural event reviews.
   - Target pace: **about 80 seconds per item**.
   - Technique: Read the question prompt first, identify key operational nouns (*motif, délai, public cible, exigence*), scan the text for synonymous lexical fields, and cross-verify.
   - **Checkpoint 2 (Clock reads 30:00 remaining)**: You must have answered item 25. You have now banked **30 full minutes** for the 14 hardest questions.

3. **Tier 3: Items 26 to 39 (Harder, Denser Texts — Budget: 30 Minutes)**:
   - Typical documents: opinion pieces, analyses, reports, commentary.
   - Target pace: **about 2 minutes per item**.
   - Watch for high-level distractors:
     - **The Absolute Trap**: Options containing *toujours, jamais, exclusivement, totalement* when the author uses cautious modalization (*semble indiquer, tend à prouver*).
     - **The Literal Lexical Echo**: Options repeating the author's exact poetic phrasing to assert an interpretation opposite to the text.
     - **The False Friend Trap**: Watch out for words like *actuellement* (meaning *currently*, not *actually*) and *éventuellement* (meaning *potentially*, not *eventually*).
   - **Checkpoint 3 (Clock reads 10:00 remaining)**: You must reach item 35.
   - **Checkpoint 4 (Clock reads 02:00 remaining)**: Every single item from 1 to 39 must be answered. Verify that zero items are left blank: a wrong guess is not known to cost you anything (confirm in the FEI candidate guide), while a blank is a guaranteed zero for that item.

#### Scoring Rubric

Compare any score estimate from your practice tool with the IRCC equivalency table for TCF Canada. There is **no official conversion from a count of correct answers out of 39 to a score out of 699**, because items are weighted by difficulty, so don't try to convert your raw count yourself; use the number of correct answers only to track your own progress from one sprint to the next.

| NCLC | Compréhension orale (/699) | Compréhension écrite (/699) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 10+ | 549–699 | 549–699 |
| 9 | 523–548 | 524–548 |
| 8 | 503–522 | 499–523 |
| **7 (TARGET)** | **458–502** | **453–498** |
| 6 | 398–457 | 406–452 |
| 5 | 369–397 | 375–405 |
| 4 | 331–368 | 342–374 |

**What NCLC 7 means for Express Entry:** NCLC 7 is the **threshold** for the CRS French bonus, not "maximum CRS points" (language points keep rising up to CLB 10). The bonus requires **NCLC 7 or higher in all four French skills**: it is **50 points** if your English is CLB 5 or higher, and **25 points** if your English is CLB 4 or lower (the two don't stack; the maximum is 50).

#### Extra credit

Candidates aiming for a comfortable safety margin above NCLC 7 can complete two high-intensity extension drills:

1. **The Compressed Reading Drill (45-Minute Limit)**: Take a fresh 39-question reading test with an accelerated countdown timer set to **45 minutes** instead of 60 minutes. Matching your usual 60-minute result under a 45-minute limit makes normal test conditions feel spacious and calm.
2. **The Transcript Reconstruction Audit**: Take the three most difficult listening audio items you missed during the sprint. Listen to each audio snippet three times and transcribe every spoken word by hand in French. Compare your transcription with the transcript provided with the practice material to identify exactly which spoken reduction (such as **« ch'sais pas »** for *je ne sais pas*, or **« y a »** for *il y a*) or Canadian phonetic feature caused the comprehension blackout.
