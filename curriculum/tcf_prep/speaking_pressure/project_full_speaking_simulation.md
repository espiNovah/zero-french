### Introduction

Welcome to the capstone project of **Speaking Under Pressure & Spontaneous Interaction** in **Path 04: TCF Canada Exam Prep**.

Throughout the preceding four lessons of this module, you systematically deconstructed the psychological, tactical, and linguistic demands of the face-to-face oral examination:
1. **Task 1 Modular Intro Framework**: You built a four-module self-presentation (Identity, Trajectory, Passions, Canadian Project) that delivers an articulate B2/C1 profile in about 60–75 seconds and handles unexpected examiner questions.
2. **Task 2 Two-Minute Prep Template**: You mastered the 6-W + 4-T shorthand matrix, generating a full set of question triggers in your notes in about 90 seconds and leading the 3.5-minute interactive roleplay with conversational rebounds.
3. **Task 3 Sustaining the Monologue**: You internalized the five-phase mental roadmap and the "Rule of Three" analytical lenses, sustaining an unscripted argument for four minutes and thirty seconds with Canadian societal anchoring.
4. **Overcoming Hesitations & Recovery**: You replaced vocalized pauses (*euh*) with high-register rhetorical fillers, learned to climb the Circumlocution Ladder during lexical amnesia, and mastered surgical self-repair without apologizing.

Now, you must bring all three tasks together in a **full 12-minute, continuous oral examination simulation** under exam-like conditions.

In the TCF Canada, the **Expression orale** test lasts **12 minutes**, face to face with an examiner, and receives one overall score out of 20. There are no retries and no script consultations. A score of **10–11/20 corresponds to NCLC 7** on the IRCC equivalency table. For Express Entry, the French-language bonus requires **NCLC 7 or higher in all four French skills**: it is worth **50 CRS points** if your English is CLB 5 or higher, and 25 points if your English is CLB 4 or lower. NCLC 7 is a threshold, not the maximum: CRS language points keep rising above it.

### Before you start

To produce an authentic, audit-grade baseline of your oral performance, configure your practice environment to mimic exam conditions:

- **Audio Recording Setup**: Use an audio recorder (smartphone voice memo application, Audacity, or an online recorder such as Vocaroo). The recording must run continuously for the entire **12 minutes and 00 seconds** without pausing.
- **Physical Tools**: Place a single blank sheet of paper and a pencil on your desk for Task 2 preparation notes. No dictionaries, notebooks, or typed notes.
- **Timer**: Have a digital countdown timer ready to regulate each task transition.
- **Physical Demeanor**: Sit upright at a desk in a quiet room. Speak at a confident, clearly projected volume. Imagine an official examiner sitting directly across from you.
- **Unbroken Continuity**: Once you press "Record," you must complete Task 1, Task 2, and Task 3 sequentially without stopping, restarting, or re-recording any section.

#### The 12-Minute Master Speaking Protocol

Execute the simulation according to this strict chronological breakdown:

| Exam Phase | Duration | Description |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Task 1: Entretien dirigé** | **2 minutes** | Spontaneous self-introduction + examiner follow-up interaction (no preparation). |
| **Task 2: Exercice en interaction** | **5 minutes 30** | Interactive roleplay: **2 minutes preparation** (notes allowed) + **3 minutes 30 seconds speaking**. |
| **Task 3: Expression d'un point de vue** | **4 minutes 30** | Argued point of view on a societal question (no preparation); the examiner may intervene. |

#### Task 1: Entretien dirigé (2 Minutes | No Preparation)

**Examiner Opening Prompt**:
> **« Bonjour, installez-vous. Nous allons commencer l'épreuve d'Expression orale par la première tâche. Pouvez-vous vous présenter, me parler de vous, de votre parcours professionnel et de ce qui motive votre projet d'immigration vers le Canada ? »**

**Candidate Action**:
1. Deliver your four-module introduction fluidly (approx. 60–75 seconds):
   - Module 1: Identity & Professional Specialization (20s).
   - Module 2: Career Milestone or Key Responsibility (25s).
   - Module 3: Personal Passion, Sport, or Civic Commitment (20s).
   - Module 4: The Canadian Project & Francophone Integration (15s).
2. At the 1:15 mark, pause and respond to this simulated examiner follow-up:
   > **« Vous avez mentionné votre expérience professionnelle. Si vous deviez mettre en avant une compétence clé qui facilitera votre intégration sur le marché du travail canadien, laquelle choisiriez-vous et pourquoi ? »**
3. Answer the follow-up with a conversational bridge formula (*« C'est un aspect fondamental... »*) for the remaining 40 seconds.
4. Conclude cleanly as the timer reaches **02:00**.

---

#### Task 2: Exercice en interaction (5 Minutes 30 Seconds Total)

**Phase 1: Silent Preparation (2 Minutes Sharp)**:
Start your 2-minute preparation countdown. Read the practice prompt card below and write shorthand notes on your scrap paper using the 6-W + 4-T grid. Do not write complete sentences:

> **Fiche consigne — Tâche 2** :  
> Vous souhaitez vous inscrire à un club de randonnée et d'activités de plein air qui organise des sorties de fin de semaine dans les parcs nationaux.  
> Vous rencontrez le responsable de l'association pour obtenir des informations détaillées avant de devenir membre.  
> Vous lui posez des questions pour vous renseigner sur :  
> - Le calendrier des sorties et les niveaux de difficulté proposés.  
> - Les modalités d'hébergement et le matériel nécessaire (fourni vs personnel).  
> - Le coût de l'adhésion annuelle et ce qui est inclus dans le forfait.  
> - Les règles de sécurité et les conditions d'annulation en cas de mauvais temps.  
> *(L'examinateur joue le rôle du responsable du club).*

**Phase 2: Live Roleplay (3 Minutes 30 Seconds)**:
When the 2-minute preparation timer expires, begin speaking immediately. Maintain conversational leadership:
1. Deliver your warm opening hook: *« Bonjour Monsieur/Madame, je viens vous voir concernant l'annonce... »*
2. Ask a varied set of structured questions (around ten is a good personal practice target, not an exam rule), alternating your syntax (polite inversion, indirect questions, *est-ce que*).
3. Offer natural conversational rebounds to imagined examiner answers (*« C'est une excellente nouvelle », « D'accord, je prends bonne note », « Très bien, et concernant... »*).
4. **Negotiate this obstacle**: Imagine the examiner tells you that the next three weekend excursions are fully booked for beginners. Negotiate a solution (ask about a waiting list, intermediate outings, or solo preparation workshops).
5. Conclude with a polite reservation commitment around **minute 5:15 to 5:30**.

---

#### Task 3: Expression d'un point de vue (4 Minutes 30 Seconds | No Preparation)

**Examiner Prompt**:
> **« Nous passons maintenant à la troisième tâche. Vous devez exprimer votre point de vue de manière argumentée et structurée sur le sujet suivant :  
> L'école moderne prépare-t-elle adéquatement les jeunes aux réalités du monde contemporain ?  
> Je pourrai vous poser quelques questions pendant votre exposé. À vous. »**

**Candidate Action**:
Begin speaking right away using the 5-phase "Rule of Three" architecture:
1. **Phase 1: Hook, Problematic & Plan (0:00 – 0:45)**: Frame the educational dilemma (traditional academic transmission vs practical 21st-century skills). Announce your three-part trajectory.
2. **Phase 2: Pillar 1 — The Academic & Theoretical Foundation (0:45 – 1:45)**: Discuss the enduring value of foundational knowledge (mathematics, literacy, critical thinking, history) in fostering intellectual autonomy.
3. **Phase 3: Pillar 2 — The Practical & Technological Lag (1:45 – 2:45)**: Highlight systemic shortcomings: insufficient preparation for digital literacy, artificial intelligence, financial management, emotional resilience, and civic engagement.
4. **Phase 4: Pillar 3 — Societal Nuance & Equal Opportunity (2:45 – 3:45)**: Analyze the school's role as an engine of social mobility, pointing out how socio-economic disparities and unequal access to modern educational resources threaten this ideal.
5. **Phase 5: Synthesis, Verdict & Future Perspective (3:45 – 4:30)**: Summarize your argument, propose constructive educational reforms (project-based learning, hybrid curricula), and conclude with an open forward-looking reflection.
6. Stop speaking at **4 minutes 25 seconds**. (In this solo run, you can also ask a partner to drop in one or two questions — *« Pouvez-vous préciser ? », « Et si on vous objectait que… ? »* — as the real examiner may do.)

#### Scoring Rubric

This is an **unofficial self-assessment rubric** built for this course; it is not FEI's grid. The real Expression orale receives **one overall score out of 20** — there are no published per-task or per-criterion scores. Use the rows below as a checklist, then give yourself one global estimate out of 20:

| Self-Check Area | Task 1 | Task 2 | Task 3 | What a strong performance looks like |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Task completion** | Fluently presents identity, career, hobbies, and Canadian project; answers follow-up without stalling. | Asks a varied set of relevant questions covering logistics, costs, and terms; negotiates obstacle gracefully. | Sustains monologue for 4:15–4:30; delivers balanced introduction, 3 pillars, and formal conclusion. | Full adherence to time limits; addresses all prompt facets with maturity and depth. |
| **2. Interaction & organization** | Natural conversational turn-taking; appropriate greetings and professional demeanor. | Dynamic roleplay leadership; seamless conversational rebounds (*« c'est bon à savoir »*); polite closing. | Logical, elegant discourse progression; high-register articulators (*force est de reconnaître, en dernière analyse*). | Smooth transitions, zero awkward pauses, immediate recovery from conversational friction. |
| **3. Vocabulary range & control** | Varied biographical and professional vocabulary; avoids simplistic A2 formulas (*j'aime, je veux*). | Rich administrative, financial, and logistical vocabulary suited to sports/outdoor clubs. | Abstract sociological, pedagogical, and technological lexicon; precise collocations; minimal repetition. | Wide lexical range; precise and idiomatic B2/C1 terms; successful circumlocution during memory lapses. |
| **4. Grammar & pronunciation** | Accurate verb tenses (passé composé, imparfait, présent); clean vowel pronunciation and liaisons. | Varied interrogative syntax (inversion, indirect clauses, polite conditional); flawless gender agreements. | Complex syntactic structures (concession, hypothesis, passive); natural intonation and cadence. | High degree of grammatical control; minor slips are self-repaired; clear, easily intelligible phonetics. |

#### Score /20 → NCLC (IRCC Equivalency Table)

| Expression orale score (/20) | NCLC |
| :---: | :---: |
| **16 – 20** | NCLC 10+ |
| **14 – 15** | NCLC 9 |

| **10 – 11** | **NCLC 7 (target)** |
| **7 – 9** | NCLC 6 |

NCLC 7 in **all four** French skills is the threshold for the Express Entry French bonus (50 CRS points with English at CLB 5+, 25 points with English at CLB 4 or lower). Your self-estimate is only indicative; aim for 12/20 or more in practice to leave a margin.

For candidates who want a safety margin above NCLC 7 (12/20 or more):
