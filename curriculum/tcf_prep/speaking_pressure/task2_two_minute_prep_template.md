### Introduction

In the **Expression orale** section of the TCF Canada, **Task 2 (l'exercice en interaction)** is where candidates frequently experience their most acute time crisis. Unlike Task 1 and Task 3, which have no preparation time, Task 2 grants you **two minutes of preparation, during which you can take notes**, followed by **three minutes and thirty seconds of live, interactive roleplay with the examiner**.

The task prompt presents a realistic situational card: you are asking about renting a cottage, enrolling in a language school, joining a volunteer sports league, booking a tour, or hiring a home service. Your job is to **take the initiative, conduct the exchange, ask relevant questions to obtain the information you need, react naturally to the examiner's answers, and handle any obstacles that come up**. No minimum number of questions is published; asking a varied, well-connected set matters more than hitting a count.

Untrained candidates suffer from **full-sentence paralysis during the two-minute prep window**:
- They panic and attempt to write out entire, word-for-word French questions on their scrap paper: *« Est-ce que vous pourriez me dire à quelle heure commence le... »*.
- By the time the examiner announces that the two minutes have elapsed, the candidate has written down only three questions.
- During the roleplay, they stare downward at their sheet, read their three questions robotically, and freeze in silence by minute 1:30.

To dominate Task 2, you must abandon full-sentence drafting and master the **Shorthand Matrix (The 6-W + 4-T Grid)**. This rapid note-taking template generates **12 to 15 diverse question triggers in under 90 seconds**, freeing the remaining 30 seconds to mentally rehearse your opening hook and formal conversational rebounds.

---

### Lesson overview

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Maximize the **strict two-minute preparation window** using visual scrap-paper shorthand instead of handwriting complete sentences.
- Deploy the **10-Category Question Generation Matrix (6-W + 4-T)** to instantly brainstorm inquiries covering logistics, costs, terms, prerequisites, and contingencies.
- Vary your syntactic question structures across four grammatical registers: formal subject-verb inversion, polite conditional indirect questions, *est-ce que*, and intonation.
- Command the **3.5-minute roleplay interaction**, taking the conversational lead, offering natural interactive rebounds (*« Très bien », « C'est bon à savoir », « En effet »*), and handling any obstacles the examiner raises.
- Conclude the interaction gracefully with a professional booking commitment or polite reservation.

### The 2-minute prep pressure: Scrap paper shorthand vs full-sentence paralysis

On test day you receive the task instructions and two minutes to prepare. The exact wording and materials depend on the centre, but the instruction may sound something like this:
> **« Vous avez deux minutes pour préparer cette tâche. Vous pouvez prendre des notes. Vous devez me poser des questions pour obtenir des informations. À vous. »**  
> — /vu.z‿a.ve dø mi.nyt puʁ pʁe.pa.ʁe sɛt tɑʃ. vu pu.ve pʁɑ̃dʁ de nɔt. vu də.ve mə po.ze de kɛs.tjɔ̃ puʁ ɔb.tə.niʁ de.z‿ɛ̃.fɔʁ.ma.sjɔ̃. a vu/

Two minutes is **120 seconds**. If you attempt to handwrite complete grammatical sentences, you write at approximately 15 words per minute. Three complete questions will consume your entire preparation time.

#### The Scrap Paper Protocol: Symbols and Keywords Only

Your notes are not scored. Only what you actually say during the interaction counts.

Divide your scrap paper into three visual quadrants in the first 10 seconds:

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| CADRANT 1: L'ACCROCHE (5 sec)                                     |
| "Bonjour / annonce club / intéressé / qqs questions"              |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| CADRANT 2: LA MATRICE DES QUESTIONS (75 sec)                      |
| [1] QUAND ?    (dates / horaires / début session)                 |
| [2] OÙ ?       (adresse / accès métro / stationnement)            |
| [3] QUI ?      (public / niveau débutant / taille groupe)         |
| [4] QUOI ?     (programme / activités / matériel fourni ?)        |
| [5] POURQUOI ? (objectifs / débouchés / certification ?)          |
| [6] COMMENT ?  (modalités d'inscription / test d'entrée ?)        |
| [7] COÛT ?     (tarif mensuel / réduction étudiant / taxes ?)     |
| [8] CONDITIONS?(engagement / politique annulation / caution ?)    |
| [9] MATÉRIEL ? (équipement spécial / tenue exigée ?)              |
| [10] IMPRÉVUS? (remboursement en cas de maladie ?)                |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| CADRANT 3: LA CONCLUSION (10 sec)                                 |
| "Parfait / inscription / coordonnées / merci"                     |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

By recording only single-word triggers (*COÛT, ANNULATION, MATÉRIEL*), your eyes merely glance at your scrap paper during the interaction, allowing you to maintain direct eye contact with the examiner.

### The 10-category question generation matrix (6-W + 4-T)

To ensure that your questions never dry up during the 3.5 minutes, memorize the **6-W + 4-T Matrix**. This universal framework applies to any prompt card imaginable (housing, sports, tourism, education, volunteering, consumer services).

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| THE 6-W CORE LOGISTICAL QUESTIONS                                 |
| 1. QUAND (Timing)       : Dates, horaires, fréquence, durée       |
| 2. OÙ (Location)        : Emplacement, accessibilité, transport   |
| 3. QUI (Participants)   : Profil, âge, encadrement, taille groupe |
| 4. QUOI (Content)       : Activités, modules, options proposées   |
| 5. POURQUOI (Purpose)   : Avantages, certification, objectifs     |
| 6. COMMENT (Procedure)  : Démarches, prérequis, formulaire        |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                  +
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| THE 4-T PRAGMATIC CONSTRAINTS                                     |
| 7. TARIFS (Financial)   : Frais d'adhésion, forfaits, remises     |
| 8. TERMES (Legal/Rules) : Contrat, engagement, caution, règles    |
| 9. TENUE (Equipment)    : Matériel requis vs fourni sur place     |
| 10. TOLÉRANCE (Risk)    : Remboursement, reports, annulation      |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

#### Transforming Shorthand Keywords into Advanced Syntactic Structures

To achieve a B2 or C1 score, you must vary your question syntax. Repeating **« Est-ce que vous avez... ? Est-ce que c'est... ? Est-ce que je peux... ? »** ten times makes your range look limited and holds your performance at a B1 impression.

Deploy these four distinct grammatical mechanisms across the interview:

| Syntactic Register | Grammatical Formula | Concrete Exam Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Polite Conditional Inversion** | Verb + Hyphen + Subject Pronoun | **« Pourriez-vous me préciser les modalités de règlement ? »** — /pu.ʁje vu mə pʁe.si.ze le mɔ.da.li.te də ʁɛ.ɡlə.mɑ̃/ |
| **2. Indirect Embedded Question** | J'aimerais savoir si / ce que / quand... | **« J'aimerais savoir si le matériel pédagogique est fourni gracieusement. »** — /ʒ‿ɛ.mə.ʁɛ sa.vwaʁ si lə ma.te.ʁjɛl pe.da.ɡɔ.ʒik ɛ fuʁ.ni ɡʁa.sjøz.mɑ̃/ |
| **3. Complex Interrogative Phrase** | Qu'en est-il de... / Dans quelle mesure... | **« Qu'en est-il de la politique de remboursement en cas de force majeure ? »** — /k‿ɑ̃n‿ɛt‿il də la pɔ.li.tik də ʁɑ̃.buʁ.sə.mɑ̃ ɑ̃ ka də fɔʁs ma.ʒœʁ/ |
| **4. Classical Est-ce que / Quel** | Quel(le)s sont les... | **« Quelles sont les pièces justificatives exigées lors de l'inscription ? »** — /kɛl sɔ̃ le pjɛs ʒys.ti.fi.ka.tiv.z‿ɛɡ.zi.ʒe lɔʁ də l‿ɛ̃s.kʁip.sjɔ̃/ |

### Driving the 3.5-minute roleplay: Conversational rebounds and objection negotiation

Task 2 is not a dry police interrogation. It is a simulated real-world negotiation. A candidate who simply fires off questions without acknowledging the answers shows little real interaction.

#### The 3-Step Roleplay Communication Cycle

Every question must follow a natural interactive cycle:

```
[Candidate: Poses targeted question with high-register syntax]
                       |
                       v
[Examiner: Answers, adds details, or introduces minor obstacle]
                       |
                       v
[Candidate: Conversational Rebound + Follow-up or Pivot Question]
```

#### Authentic Conversational Rebound Formulas

Never let an examiner's answer drop into silence. Use these formulas to validate the information before pivoting:

1. **When the news is favorable**:
   - **« C'est une excellente nouvelle, cela correspond parfaitement à mes attentes. »** — /s‿ɛt‿yn ɛk.sɛ.lɑ̃t nu.vɛl sə.la kɔ.ʁɛs.pɔ̃ paʁ.fɛt.mɑ̃ a me.z‿a.tɑ̃t/ (*That is excellent news; that matches my expectations perfectly.*)
   - **« C'est très avantageux. Et à ce propos... »** — /s‿ɛ tʁɛ.z‿a.vɑ̃.ta.ʒø. e a sə pʁɔ.po/ (*That is very advantageous. And speaking of which...*)
2. **When the information is neutral / factual**:
   - **« D'accord, je prends bonne note de cet horaire. »** — /da.kɔʁ ʒə pʁɑ̃ bɔn nɔt də sɛt‿ɔ.ʁɛʁ/ (*Understood, I make good note of that schedule.*)
   - **« Très bien, c'est tout à fait clair. Passons maintenant à... »** — /tʁɛ bjɛ̃ s‿ɛ tu.t‿a fɛ klɛʁ. pa.sɔ̃ mɛ̃t.nɑ̃ a/ (*Very well, that is completely clear. Moving on now to...*)
3. **When the examiner presents an objection or restriction**:
   - The examiner might say: *« Malheureusement, les cours du soir sont complets pour le mois prochain. »*
   - **Do not say**: *« Ah. Dommage. »*
   - **High-Scoring Objection Negotiation**:
     **« Je comprends tout à fait. Dans cette hypothèse, existerait-il une liste d'attente sur laquelle je pourrais m'inscrire ? À défaut, proposez-vous des créneaux équivalents le week-end ? »**  
     — /ʒə kɔ̃.pʁɑ̃ tu.t‿a fɛ. dɑ̃ sɛt i.pɔ.tɛz ɛɡ.zis.tə.ʁɛ.t‿il yn list d‿a.tɑ̃t syʁ la.kɛl ʒə pu.ʁɛ m‿ɛ̃s.kʁiʁ/

#### Québec vs France

In Task 2 roleplays situated in a Canadian context (inquiring about services, clubs, courses, or rentals), you will encounter common Canadian everyday vocabulary:
- Shopping (browsing stores, buying clothes, etc.): in France, *faire du shopping* or *faire les magasins*; in Québec, **magasiner** **(QC)**. (Grocery shopping is *faire l'épicerie* **(QC)** / *faire les courses*.)
- Parking lot: in France, *le parking*; in Québec, **le stationnement** **(QC)** (in France, *stationnement* mostly means the act of parking).
- The weekend: in France, *le week-end*; in Québec, **la fin de semaine** **(QC)**.
- Customer service staff: in France, *le vendeur* or *le conseiller*; in Québec, **le commis** **(QC)** or **le préposé** **(QC)**.
- Operating hours: in France, *les horaires d'ouverture*; in Québec, **les heures d'affaires** **(QC)** (*les heures de bureau* is used everywhere).
Both European and Canadian vocabulary are correct French, but recognizing Canadian terms helps when the scenario is set in Canada.

</div>

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--tip" markdown="1">

#### How to Signal the Conclusion of the Roleplay

When the examiner signals that time is nearly up (around the 3:15 mark), do not let the conversation trail off awkwardly. Deliver a crisp, professional conclusion:
**« Écoutez, je vous remercie infiniment pour toutes ces explications précises. Tout cela me convient parfaitement. Serait-il possible de remplir dès maintenant une fiche de pré-inscription ou préférez-vous que je repasse demain avec l'ensemble des documents ? »**

#### Step 3: Receptive Diagnostic Audit — The Passive Roleplay

Read the following transcript snippet of an interaction between an examiner and a candidate:

> **Candidat**: Est-ce qu'il y a des cours de danse ?  
> **Examinateur**: Oui, nous proposons des cours de salsa le mardi et de tango le jeudi soir.  
> **Candidat**: D'accord. Et combien ça coûte ?  
> **Examinateur**: C'est 150 euros pour un trimestre de dix séances.  
> **Candidat**: D'accord. Et où est l'école ?  
> **Examinateur**: C'est près de la gare centrale, rue de la République.  
> **Candidat**: D'accord. Et est-ce que je peux essayer ?  

**Audit Questions**:
1. What makes this interaction sound repetitive and unengaging?
2. Identify the repetitive lexical crutch used after every examiner response.
3. How would an Upper B2 candidate transform the second exchange (*« C'est 150 euros pour un trimestre... »*) to demonstrate interactive leadership?

**Diagnostic Findings**:
1. **Mechanical Question Bombardment**: The candidate acts like an interrogation bot. There is zero communicative depth or human reaction to the information provided.
2. **The « D'accord » Crutch**: Saying *« D'accord »* four times in a row demonstrates an impoverished spoken repertoire.
3. **B2 Transformation**:
   - Instead of *« D'accord. Et où est l'école ? »*, the B2 candidate says:
