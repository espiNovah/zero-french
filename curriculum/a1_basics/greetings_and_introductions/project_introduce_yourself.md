### Introduction

In this project, you will record your very first formal speaking performance: a 2-minute self-introduction that directly mirrors **Task 1 of the TCF Canada Speaking examination (*Expression orale — Entretien dirigé*)**.

In the actual TCF Canada test, Speaking Task 1 lasts **exactly 2 minutes** without any preparation time. The examiner welcomes you, introduces themselves, and asks you to speak about yourself: your identity, your background, where you live, your occupation or studies, and your interests. While this is the most accessible task on the exam, examiners immediately evaluate your pronunciation, your grammatical baseline, and your ability to maintain a natural flow of speech.

Completing this project provides your initial baseline artifact in your language portfolio—proof that you can articulate coherent spoken French under timed exam constraints.

### Before you start

Before recording, ensure you have the necessary setup ready:

- **Audio Recording Tool**: Use your smartphone's built-in voice recorder, your computer's audio app, or a free browser-based tool such as [Vocaroo](https://vocaroo.com/).
- **Countdown Timer**: A physical timer or phone stopwatch set to **2 minutes (120 seconds)**.
- **Reference Review**: Review the phrases and registers taught in [Greetings and Politeness](#/lessons/a1-basics/greetings-and-politeness) and the verb conjugations and identity forms in [Personal Identity and Essential Verbs](#/lessons/a1-basics/personal-identity-and-verbs).
- **Physical Environment**: Choose a quiet room with minimal background echo to simulate testing room conditions.

### Assignment

<div class="lesson-content__panel" markdown="1">

1. **Step 1: Draft Your Introduction Outline (No full memorization)**  
   In the real exam, you do not have notes. For this first benchmark, you may write an outline of 6–8 bullet points on paper. Do not write full sentences to memorize; use prompt keywords:
   - Salutation and formal greeting (*Bonjour, monsieur / madame*)
   - Full name (*Je m'appelle...*)
   - Country of origin and nationality (*Je viens de... / Je suis...*)
   - Current city of residence (*J'habite à...*)
   - Occupation or field of study (*Je suis ingénieur / J'étudie l'informatique*)
   - Languages spoken (*Je parle anglais et j'apprends le français*)
   - One personal passion or weekend activity (*J'aime la musique / le sport*)
   - Polite closing and thank-you (*Merci beaucoup pour votre écoute, bonne journée*)

2. **Step 2: Timed Spoken Rehearsal**  
   Read your outline once silently. Then, look away from your notes and practice speaking out loud. Focus on:
   - Starting with a clear formal greeting.
   - Using full simple sentences (*sujet + verbe + complément*).
   - Linking words smoothly (*et*, *aussi*, *mais*).
   - Pronouncing nasal vowels cleanly in words like *bonjour* (/bɔ̃.ʒuʁ/) and *français* (/fʁɑ̃.sɛ/).

3. **Step 3: The 2-Minute Recorded Take**  
   Start your timer and hit record. Deliver your self-introduction continuously. 
   - **Do not stop or restart the recording if you hesitate or make a mistake.** In the TCF exam, the audio recorder runs uninterrupted. If you stumble, pause, take a breath, correct yourself calmly, and continue.
   - Aim to speak for between 90 and 120 seconds. If you finish your prepared points early, elaborate on your city, your hobbies, or why you are learning French for Canada.

#### Evidence

Save and archive the following three items in your study directory:

- **Audio File**: Save your 2-minute recording as `speaking_task1_baseline_a1.mp3` (or `.wav`).
- **Score Log Entry**: Log your session in your master mock-exam tracking sheet with the date, duration spoken, and your self-assessed score.
- **Self-Assessment Reflection**: Write a brief 3-sentence note addressing:
  1. Did you maintain formal register (*vous*) throughout?
  2. Did you sustain speech for at least 90 seconds without prolonged silences?
  3. Which specific sounds (nasals, *r*, *u*) need focused pronunciation drill?

#### Scoring Rubric

Evaluate your recording against the official France Éducation international (FEI) criteria for TCF Speaking Task 1 (adapted for A1 baseline scoring, total: 20 points):

| Criterion | 0–1 points (Below A1) | 2–3 points (Emerging A1) | 4–5 points (Mastered A1 Benchmark) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Respect de la consigne (Task Completion)** | Speaks for less than 45 seconds; omits greeting or basic personal facts. | Speaks for 45–80 seconds; covers basic identity with substantial prompting or long pauses. | Speaks for 90–120 seconds; covers identity, origin, residence, and profession with appropriate opening and closing. |
| **2. Cohérence et cohésion (Organization)** | Isolated words or disjointed fragments; no logical link between statements. | Simple phrases linked primarily by basic repetition (*et... et...*). | Clearly structured progression; uses basic connectors (*et*, *mais*, *aussi*) to sequence ideas. |
| **3. Lexique (Vocabulary)** | Extremely limited vocabulary; frequent resort to English words. | Basic repertoire of everyday words sufficient for simple personal facts. | Varied, accurate A1 vocabulary for greetings, nationality, professions, and hobbies. |
| **4. Morphosyntaxe et phonétique (Grammar & Pronunciation)** | Unintelligible pronunciation; pervasive verb errors preventing comprehension. | Pronunciation requires listener effort; basic verbs (*être*, *avoir*, *s'appeler*) partially accurate. | Pronunciation is comprehensible; clear distinction between vowels; basic present tense forms used correctly. |

**Score Conversion**:
- **0–7 points**: Below A1
- **8–14 points**: Solid A1 (NCLC 1–2 equivalent)
- **15–20 points**: Strong A1 / Transition to A2 (NCLC 3 equivalent)

### Submitting your work

A successful submission demonstrates:

1. **Audio Integrity**: A continuous, unedited recording of at least 90 seconds.
2. **Polite Framing**: Begins with *Bonjour* and concludes with *Merci, bonne journée / Au revoir*.
3. **Honest Self-Assessment**: An objective review using the rubric above, with targets identified for upcoming lessons on present tense verb conjugations.
