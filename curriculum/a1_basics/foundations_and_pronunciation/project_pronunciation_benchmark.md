### Introduction

In this benchmark project, you will record your very first structured oral performance dedicated to phonetics: a 3-minute diagnostic recording testing your command of the sound system of French. This project directly targets your oral pronunciation baseline for **all three speaking tasks of the TCF Canada (*Expression orale*)** as well as your acoustic decoding accuracy for **TCF Listening (*Compréhension orale*)**.

In the TCF Canada exam, the examiner hears your pronunciation from your very first utterance in Speaking Task 1 and throughout Tasks 2 and 3, and it feeds into your single overall speaking score (/20). Pronunciation errors that obscure meaning—such as confusing **/y/** (in *tu*) with **/u/** (in *tout*), pronouncing silent verb endings like *-ent*, or dropping mandatory liaisons—make you harder to understand and hold your score back. Furthermore, candidates who cannot articulate these phonemes reliably consistently struggle to recognize them when listening to fast-paced native recordings in the 35-minute Listening test.

Completing this project creates a permanent acoustic reference point in your portfolio. You will record minimal pairs, nasal vowel contrasts, silent consonant rules, mandatory liaisons, and high-frequency number sequences, and audit your delivery against an unofficial self-assessment rubric.

### Before you start

Before beginning your recording session, ensure you have the following setup prepared:

- **Audio Recording Tool**: Use your smartphone voice recorder, a desktop audio application (such as QuickTime, Audacity, or Voice Memos), or the free browser tool [Vocaroo](https://vocaroo.com/).
- **Microphone & Headset**: Use a wired or quality wireless headset to ensure clear vocal capture without room reverberation.
- **Countdown Timer**: A stopwatch or phone timer set to **3 minutes (180 seconds)**.
- **Reference Review**: If needed, quickly review the phonetic rules taught in:
  - [The Alphabet, Accents, and Spelling](#/lessons/a1-basics/alphabet-and-accents)
  - [Pure Vowels, U vs OU, and Nasal Sounds](#/lessons/a1-basics/vowels-and-nasals)
  - [Silent Letters, Elision, and Liaisons](#/lessons/a1-basics/silent-letters-and-liaisons)
  - [Numbers, Time, and the Calendar](#/lessons/a1-basics/numbers-time-dates)
- **Practice Reading**: Read the benchmark script once silently and once at half-speed out loud to loosen your articulatory muscles before recording your final take.

1. **Step 1: Study the 5-Part Pronunciation Benchmark Script**  
   The text below is specifically engineered to test the five most high-frequency phonetic pitfalls for English speakers on the TCF Canada:

   > **Partie 1 : Voyelles orales et paires minimales (/y/ vs /u/, /e/ vs /ɛ/)**  
   > 1. *Tu as tout lu.* — /ty a tu ly/ — (You read everything.)  
   > 2. *Il a bu un café sous la tente.* — /i.l‿a by œ̃ ka.fe su la tɑ̃t/ — (He drank a coffee under the tent.)  
   > 3. *J'ai répété les règles après la fête.* — /ʒe ʁe.pe.te le ʁɛ.ɡl‿a.pʁɛ la fɛt/ — (I repeated the rules after the party.)  
   > 4. *Le dessert était sur la table du chef.* — /lə de.sɛʁ e.tɛ syʁ la tabl dy ʃɛf/ — (The dessert was on the chef's table.)  
   > 
   > **Partie 2 : Voyelles nasales en contraste (/ɑ̃/, /ɔ̃/, /ɛ̃/)**  
   > 5. *Mon grand cousin blond mange du bon pain blanc.* — /mɔ̃ ɡʁɑ̃ ku.zɛ̃ blɔ̃ mɑ̃ʒ dy bɔ̃ pɛ̃ blɑ̃/ — (My tall blond cousin eats good white bread.)  
   > 6. *En automne, cent Canadiens dansent dans un grand restaurant.* — /ɑ̃.n‿o.tɔn sɑ̃ ka.na.djɛ̃ dɑ̃s dɑ̃.z‿œ̃ ɡʁɑ̃ ʁɛs.to.ʁɑ̃/ — (In autumn, one hundred Canadians dance in a big restaurant.)  
   > 7. *Ce bon vin blanc vient de la région du mont Saint-Michel.* — /sə bɔ̃ vɛ̃ blɑ̃ vjɛ̃ də la ʁe.ʒjɔ̃ dy mɔ̃ sɛ̃ mi.ʃɛl/ — (This good white wine comes from the Mount Saint-Michel region.)  
   > 
   > **Partie 3 : Lettres muettes et règle CaReFuL**  
   > 8. *Le loup gris dort près du grand lit vert.* — /lə lu ɡʁi dɔʁ pʁɛ dy ɡʁɑ̃ li vɛʁ/ — (The grey wolf sleeps near the big green bed.)  
   > 9. *Ils parlent, ils mangent et ils regardent souvent.* — /il paʁl il mɑ̃ʒ e il ʁə.ɡaʁd su.vɑ̃/ — (They speak, they eat, and they watch often.)  
   > 10. *Le chef et le serveur ont fini le chocolat noir.* — /lə ʃɛf e lə sɛʁ.vœʁ ɔ̃ fi.ni lə ʃɔ.kɔ.la nwaʁ/ — (The chef and the waiter finished the dark chocolate.)  
   > 
   > **Partie 4 : Liaisons obligatoires et élisions**  
   > 11. *Les‿enfants ont‿un grand‿ami en‿Afrique.* — /le.z‿ɑ̃.fɑ̃ ɔ̃.t‿œ̃ ɡʁɑ̃.t‿a.mi ɑ̃.n‿a.fʁik/ — (The children have a great friend in Africa. Note: *ont‿un* is an optional liaison; the other three are mandatory.)  
   > 12. *Nous‿avons deux‿ordinateurs et trois‿armoires.* — /nu.z‿a.vɔ̃ dø.z‿ɔʁ.di.na.tœʁ e tʁwa.z‿aʁ.mwaʁ/ — (We have two computers and three wardrobes.)  
   > 13. *Quand‿il arrive, on‿écoute son‿histoire.* — /kɑ̃.t‿i.l‿a.ʁiv ɔ̃.n‿e.kut sɔ̃.n‿is.twaʁ/ — (When he arrives, we listen to his story.)  
   > 
   > **Partie 5 : Nombres, indicatifs et codes postaux canadiens**  
   > 14. *Le prix est de soixante-dix-neuf dollars et quatre-vingt-onze cents.* — /lə pʁi ɛ də swa.sɑ̃t.diz.nœf dɔ.laʁ e katʁ.vɛ̃.ɔ̃z sɛnt/ — (The price is seventy-nine dollars and ninety-one cents.)  
   > 15. *Composez le 514-872-0391 (cinq-un-quatre, huit-sept-deux, zéro-trois-neuf-un).* — /sɛ̃k œ̃ katʁ | ɥit sɛt dø | ze.ʁo tʁwa nœf œ̃/ — (Dial 514-872-0391.)  
   > 16. *Mon code postal à Gatineau est J8X 3V7 (J-huit-X, trois-V-sept).* — /ʒi ɥit iks | tʁwa ve sɛt/ — (My postal code in Gatineau is J8X 3V7.)

2. **Step 2: Continuous 3-Minute Audio Take**  
   Start your timer and activate your recorder. Read the 16 sentences aloud in order from sentence 1 to sentence 16.
   - **Continuous Take**: Do not pause, slice, or restart the recording. If you mispronounce a sound, pause for one second, repeat the word cleanly, and move forward.
   - **Pacing**: Aim to complete the reading comfortably in **between 2 minutes and 2 minutes 45 seconds**. Do not rush; articulate every syllable with deliberate muscular tension.

3. **Step 3: Self-Acoustic Audit**  
   Put on your headphones and play back your recording. Follow the text with a pencil or digital document and check each sentence against the four diagnostic questions:
   - Did your lips push forward into a tight round whistle for **/y/** in *tu* and *bu*, distinct from **/u/** in *tout* and *sous*?
   - Did you keep the airflow through your nose for *pain* (/pɛ̃/), *blond* (/blɔ̃/), and *grand* (/ɡʁɑ̃/) without adding an English "ng" or pronouncing an "n/m" at the end?
   - Did you keep the **-ent** ending completely silent in *ils parlent*, *ils mangent*, and *ils regardent*?
   - Did you pronounce the liaisons with **/z/** (*les‿enfants*), **/t/** (*grand‿ami*, *quand‿il*), and **/n/** (*on‿écoute*, *son‿histoire*)?

#### Scoring Rubric

Evaluate your recording using this unofficial self-assessment rubric for the A1 phonetics benchmark (total: 20 points). It is not an FEI grid; the real TCF Canada gives one overall speaking score /20.

| Criterion | 0–1 points (Below A1) | 2–3 points (Emerging A1) | 4 points (Mastered A1 Benchmark) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Voyelles orales et distinction /y/ vs /u/** | Collapses /y/ into /u/ (*tu* sounds like *tout*); English diphthongs replace pure vowels (*café* pronounced "cay-fay"). | /y/ is distinct in isolated words but slips in connected speech (*tu as tout lu*); occasional vowel glide. | Flawless distinction between /y/ and /u/ in all minimal pairs; crisp, pure French monophthongs without English gliding. |
| **2. Voyelles nasales (/ɑ̃/, /ɔ̃/, /ɛ̃/)** | Nasal vowels replaced by oral vowels followed by English "ng" or "n" consonants; *pain* sounds like "pane" or "pang". | Two of three nasals distinct, but /ɑ̃/ and /ɔ̃/ or /ɛ̃/ overlap; occasional nasal air leakage into final consonants. | All three nasal vowels distinct and resonated cleanly in the nasal cavity with no consonant closure (*pain*, *blond*, *grand*). |
| **3. Lettres muettes et terminaisons -ent** | Final silent consonants voiced (*gris* pronounced "greess", *vert* pronounced "verte"); verb ending *-ent* pronounced as nasal. | Most final consonants silent, but slips on verb *-ent* or overcorrects CaReFuL consonants (*chef* silent). | Perfect application of CaReFuL rule; all final silent letters respected; verb *-ent* completely silent in all three sample verbs. |
| **4. Liaisons obligatoires et enchaînements** | All words pronounced with hard glottal stops; liaisons completely omitted (*les | enfants*, *deux | ordinateurs*). | Makes basic plural liaisons with /z/, but misses phonetic mutations (*quand‿il* with /d/ instead of /t/) or creates forbidden liaisons. | Flawless mandatory liaisons (*les‿enfants*, *grand‿ami* as /t/, *quand‿il* as /t/); smooth vocalic linking without glottal stops. |
| **5. Nombres complexes et codes canadiens** | Stumbles over vigesimal numbers (70s/90s); letter name errors on Canadian postal codes (G/J inversion or E/I trap). | Reads numbers with notable hesitation; minor digit inversion; postal code read slowly but mostly accurate. | Fluent, confident reading of 79 and 91; phone number read in pairs without hesitation; Canadian postal code letter names 100% accurate. |

**Score Conversion**:
- **0–9 points**: Below A1 — Articulatory habits from English dominate; re-study Section 1 lessons and re-record in 7 days.
- **10–15 points**: Solid A1 — Understandable pronunciation with minor vowel blurring; ready to continue to Section 2.
- **16–20 points**: Strong A1 / Transition to A2 — Excellent acoustic clarity; phonetic foundation is secure for TCF Canada preparation.
