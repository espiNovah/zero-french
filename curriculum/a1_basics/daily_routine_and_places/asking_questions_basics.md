### Introduction

In any language, making statements allows you to share information, but asking questions gives you control of the conversation. In daily life as an immigrant in Canada, asking questions is how you rent an apartment, find your platform at the train station, ask about bus transfers, inquire about banking fees, and clarify instructions at your job.

On the TCF Canada exam, asking questions is not simply one useful skill among many—it is the **sole focus of an entire exam section: Speaking Task 2 (*Expression orale — Exercice en interaction*)**. In this task, you are given an everyday document (such as a job posting, an apartment rental ad, or a fitness center brochure) and you have **2 minutes of preparation** to organize your thoughts, followed by **3 minutes and 30 seconds of live, interactive roleplay** where you must ask the examiner 10 to 15 pertinent questions. Candidates who only know how to answer questions inevitably freeze and fail Task 2.

In this lesson, you will master the mechanics of French interrogation. You will learn the three distinct registers for forming questions (rising intonation, the universal formula *est-ce que*, and formal subject-verb inversion), master the complete set of French question words (*qui, que, où, quand, comment, pourquoi, combien*), understand the interrogative adjective *quel*, and learn how to formulate polite, high-scoring questions tailored specifically to TCF Canada scenarios.

### The three question structures in French

English forms questions primarily by using helper auxiliary verbs ("Do you speak French?", "Are you coming?"). French does not use "do" or "does." Instead, French offers three distinct structural mechanisms to transform any declarative statement into a question, ranging from informal conversation to formal administrative speech.

Observe how the same question shifts across the three registers:

> **Vous parlez français ?** — /vu paʁ.le fʁɑ̃.sɛ/  
> *Do you speak French? (Register 1: Rising Intonation — Casual / Everyday)*

> **Est-ce que vous parlez français ?** — /ɛs.kə vu paʁ.le fʁɑ̃.sɛ/  
> *Do you speak French? (Register 2: Est-ce que — Standard Neutral)*

> **Parlez-vous français ?** — /paʁ.le vu fʁɑ̃.sɛ/  
> *Do you speak French? (Register 3: Inversion — Formal / Written / Exam)*

#### 1. Rising Intonation (L'intonation montante)
This is the simplest and most common structure in spoken French. You keep the exact word order of a normal sentence (*sujet + verbe + complément*) and simply raise the pitch of your voice at the end of the sentence:
- Statement: **Vous habitez à Montréal.** (Voice drops at the end: *You live in Montreal.*)
- Question: **Vous habitez à Montréal ?** (Voice rises sharply on the final syllable: *Do you live in Montreal?*)

#### 2. The Universal Formula: Est-ce que
**Est-ce que** (pronounced `/ɛs.kə/`) is the Swiss Army knife of French grammar. It literally translates to "is it that," but in practice it functions as an acoustic question marker:
- Statement: **Il travaille aujourd'hui.** (*He works today.*)
- Question: **Est-ce qu'il travaille aujourd'hui ?** (*Does he work today?*)

> **Critical Rule on Elision**: Before a vowel sound or silent *h*, *est-ce que* contracts to **est-ce qu'**:
> - *Est-ce que + il* → **Est-ce qu'il** (/ɛs.kil/)
> - *Est-ce que + elle* → **Est-ce qu'elle** (/ɛs.kɛl/)
> - *Est-ce que + on* → **Est-ce qu'on** (/ɛs.kɔ̃/)

#### 3. Subject-Verb Inversion (L'inversion)
In formal French, questions are formed by swapping the positions of the conjugated verb and the subject pronoun, joining them with a hyphen:
- Statement: **Vous avez une réservation.** (*You have a reservation.*)
- Question: **Avez-vous une réservation ?** (*Do you have a reservation?*)

When the 3rd-person singular verb ends in a vowel (*a*, *e*) and the pronoun begins with a vowel (*il*, *elle*, *on*), French inserts a euphonic **-t-** between hyphens to prevent awkward vowel clashing:
- *Il a un stylo* → **A-t-il un stylo ?** (/a.til œ̃ sti.lo/)
- *Elle habite ici* → **Habite-t-elle ici ?** (/a.bit.tɛl i.si/)

| Question Method | Structural Pattern | Register & Context | Tone & Delivery |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Intonation** | Subject + Verb + ? | Casual / Friends / Everyday spoken | Voice rises at the end of the sentence |
| **Est-ce que** | Est-ce que + Subject + Verb + ? | Standard / Professional / Neutral | Balanced, clear question marker |
| **Inversion** | Verb-Subject + ? | Formal / Administrative / High-register | Elegant; standard in customer service |

### Closed questions and answering with oui, non, and si

A closed question (une question fermée) is one that requires a simple yes-or-no answer. In English, you answer with "yes" or "no." In French, however, there are three fundamental answer words: **oui**, **non**, and **si**.

Observe the difference:

> **Vous aimez le café ? — Oui, j'aime le café.**  
> *Do you like coffee? — Yes, I like coffee.*

> **Vous habitez à Toronto ? — Non, j'habite à Montréal.**  
> *Do you live in Toronto? — No, I live in Montreal.*

> **Vous n'aimez pas l'hiver ? — Si, j'adore l'hiver !**  
> *Don't you like winter? — Yes (on the contrary), I love winter!*

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--critical" markdown="1">

#### Critical Grammar Rule: When to Use "Si"

English speakers constantly make the mistake of using *oui* to contradict a negative question. In French:
- **Oui** (/wi/): Confirms a **positive** question (*Tu viens ? — Oui.*)
- **Non** (/nɔ̃/): Denies either a positive or negative question (*Tu viens ? — Non.* / *Tu ne viens pas ? — Non.*)
- **Si** (/si/): Contradicts a **negative** question, asserting the positive (*Tu ne parles pas français ? — **Si**, je parle français !*).

Never answer a negative question with *oui*. If someone asks *"Vous n'êtes pas canadien ?"* and you are Canadian, saying *"Oui"* is confusing; you must say **"Si, je suis canadien."**

</div>

### Core question words: Who, what, where, when, and how

When you need specific information rather than a simple yes-or-no, you use open questions formed with interrogative adverbs and pronouns.

Observe how these question words function in both *est-ce que* and inverted structures:

> **Où est-ce que vous habitez ?** — /u ɛs.kə vu.z‿a.bi.te/  
> *Where do you live? (Standard neutral with est-ce que)*

> **Où habitez-vous ?** — /u a.bi.te vu/  
> *Where do you live? (Formal with inversion)*

> **Quand commence le concert ?** — /kɑ̃ kɔ.mɑ̃s lə kɔ̃.sɛʁ/  
> *When does the concert start?*

Here are the essential interrogatives every candidate must master:

| Question Word | IPA | English | Example with Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Qui** | /ki/ | Who / Whom | **Qui est à l'appareil ?** — *Who is on the phone?* |
| **Où** | /u/ | Where | **Où se trouve la station de métro ?** — *Where is the subway station located?* |
| **Quand** | /kɑ̃/ | When | **Quand arrive le train pour Ottawa ?** — *When does the train for Ottawa arrive?* |
| **Comment** | /kɔ.mɑ̃/ | How / What | **Comment vous appelez-vous ?** — *What is your name?* |
| **Que / Qu'est-ce que** | /kə/, /kɛs.kə/ | What | **Qu'est-ce que vous cherchez ?** — *What are you looking for?* |
| **Quoi** | /kwa/ | What (after preposition or at end) | **Vous parlez de quoi ?** — *What are you talking about?* |

> **Phonetic Note on Quand**: When *quand* is followed by a vowel in liaison, the silent *d* is pronounced as a clean **/t/** sound:
> - **Quand arrive-t-il ?** is pronounced `/kɑ̃.t‿a.ʁiv.til/`.

### Asking about quantities and reasons: Combien and Pourquoi

Two of the most frequent question words on immigration documents and practical tasks are **combien** (how much / how many) and **pourquoi** (why).

#### 1. Combien (de): Price and Quantity
- To ask the price of an item or service, use **C'est combien ?** (/sɛ kɔ̃.bjɛ̃/) or **Combien coûte... ?** (/kɔ̃.bjɛ̃ kut/):
  - **C'est combien pour une nuit ?** (*How much is it for one night?*)
  - **Combien coûte ce manteau ?** (*How much does this coat cost?*)
- To ask about a quantity of countable items, use **combien de** (or **combien d'** before a vowel) followed by a plural noun:
  - **Combien de pièces y a-t-il dans l'appartement ?** (*How many rooms are there in the apartment?*)
  - **Combien d'enfants avez-vous ?** (*How many children do you have?*)

#### 2. Pourquoi and Parce que: Asking and Giving Reasons
When inquiring about motivations, causes, or explanations, use **pourquoi** (/puʁ.kwa/). When answering, always respond with **parce que** (/paʁs kə/) or **parce qu'** before vowels:

> **Pourquoi apprenez-vous le français ?**  
> /puʁ.kwa a.pʁə.ne vu lə fʁɑ̃.sɛ/  
> *Why are you learning French?*

> **Parce que je vais m'installer au Canada.**  
> /paʁs kə ʒə vɛ mɛ̃s.ta.le o ka.na.da/  
> *Because I am going to settle in Canada.*

### The specific interrogative adjective: Quel and its agreements

When you want to ask "which" or "what" specifically regarding a noun (*what time*, *what address*, *which documents*), French uses the interrogative adjective **quel**.

Because *quel* is an adjective, it must **agree in gender and number** with the noun it modifies:

| Form | Gender & Number | IPA Pronunciation | Example in Context | English Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **quel** | Masculine Singular | /kɛl/ | **Quel est votre nom ?** | What is your name? |
| **quelle** | Feminine Singular | /kɛl/ | **Quelle est votre adresse ?** | What is your address? |
| **quels** | Masculine Plural | /kɛl/ | **Quels documents sont nécessaires ?**| Which documents are required? |
| **quelles** | Feminine Plural | /kɛl/ | **Quelles sont vos disponibilités ?** | What is your availability? |

Notice that all four spellings (*quel, quelle, quels, quelles*) have the **exact same pronunciation**: **/kɛl/**! While pronunciation is identical in speaking, spelling agreement is strictly evaluated in TCF Writing Task 1 and 2.

#### Québec vs France

In spoken Canadian French (particularly in everyday informal speech in Québec), you will very frequently hear questions formed with the interrogative particle **-tu** placed right after the verb:
- *Tu viens-tu ?* **(QC)** = *Est-ce que tu viens ?* (*Are you coming?*)
- *C'est-tu ouvert ?* **(QC)** = *Est-ce que c'est ouvert ?* (*Is it open?*)
- *Il fait-tu froid dehors ?* **(QC)** = *Est-ce qu'il fait froid dehors ?* (*Is it cold outside?*)

This *-tu* particle is not the pronoun *tu*; it is a historical spoken marker that signals a yes/no question. On the TCF Canada exam, you will hear this in authentic Canadian audio recordings (Listening items), but in your own Speaking and Writing tests, you should stick to standard **est-ce que** or **inversion**.

### TCF Speaking Task 2: The question blueprint

In **Speaking Task 2 (*Exercice en interaction*)**, you must maintain an active roleplay for **3 minutes and 30 seconds**. The examiner plays a receptionist, landlord, store clerk, or tour organizer, and will answer your questions briefly to see if you can lead the interaction.

To generate 10 to 15 questions effortlessly under pressure, train yourself to use the **"7-Angle Question Grid"**:

1. **Location / Address (Où)**:  
   *Où se trouve votre établissement ?* (Where is your establishment located?)  
   *Quelle est l'adresse exacte ?* (What is the exact address?)

2. **Schedule / Timing (Quand / À quelle heure)**:  
   *Quels sont vos horaires d'ouverture ?* (What are your opening hours?)  
   *À quelle heure commence le cours ?* (At what time does the class start?)

3. **Cost / Payment (Combien / Prix)**:  
   *Quel est le prix pour un adulte ?* (What is the price for an adult?)  
   *Combien coûte l'abonnement mensuel ?* (How much is the monthly subscription?)  
   *Quels sont les modes de paiement acceptés ?* (What payment methods are accepted?)

4. **Requirements / Conditions (Quels documents)**:  
   *Quels documents dois-je fournir ?* (Which documents must I provide?)  
   *Faut-il réserver à l'avance ?* (Is it necessary to book in advance?)

5. **Equipment / Amenities (Est-ce qu'il y a)**:  
   *Est-ce qu'il y a un stationnement / un parking sur place ?* (Is there parking on site?)  
   *Est-ce que le matériel est inclus ?* (Is the equipment included?)

6. **Reductions / Discounts (Réductions)**:  
   *Proposez-vous des tarifs réduits pour les étudiants ou les nouveaux arrivants ?* (Do you offer reduced rates for students or newcomers?)

7. **Contact / Confirmation**:  
   *Avez-vous un site web ou une brochure d'information ?* (Do you have a website or info brochure?)

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--warning" markdown="1">

#### Exam tip

In Speaking Task 2, do not ask the same question twice or ask questions that are already answered in the written prompt. If the prompt states *"Loyer: 950$ par mois"*, do not ask *"Combien coûte le loyer ?"* Instead, ask: *"Est-ce que le chauffage et l'électricité sont inclus dans le loyer de 950$ ?"* This demonstrates genuine reading comprehension and natural interaction.
