### Introduction

The Reading Comprehension (*Compréhension écrite*) section of the **TCF Canada** tests your ability to rapidly process everyday written French without a dictionary. In official test conditions, you must answer **39 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes**. The questions get progressively harder, from **Level A1 up to Level C2**, so the early items typically assess your functional mastery of short, real-world documents: public signage, caution posters, workplace memos, store hours, classified ads, and municipal service announcements.

Achieving a high score on the TCF Canada reading module is largely a race against the clock. Lingering too long on an easy A1 or A2 question deprives you of the cognitive reserves and time required to decode long B2 and C1 analytical editorials later in the test. To maximize your score, you must master two essential reading competencies:
1. **Écrémage (*Skimming*)**: Rapidly identifying the document type, the author, the target audience, and the overarching communicative purpose within 5 to 10 seconds.
2. **Repérage (*Scanning*)**: Targeting specific factual data (dates, addresses, monetary costs, eligibility rules, prohibitions) directly triggered by the question stem, without reading word-for-word.

In this lesson, you will analyze the visual layout and lexicon of authentic Francophone signage, dissect classified advertisements and administrative bulletins from Quebec and France, and train systematic elimination techniques to bypass common distractor traps.

This section contains a general overview of topics that you will learn in this lesson.

### Anatomy of the TCF Canada reading comprehension module

The TCF Canada reading test has **39 multiple-choice items in 60 minutes**, scored on a 0–699 scale. The one structural principle you can rely on is that **early items are easier and later items are harder**. FEI does not publish an item-number-to-level map, and items are weighted by difficulty, so treat the picture below as a general guide, not an official breakdown:

| Part of the Test | Typical Difficulty | Typical Documents (illustrative) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Early items** | **A1–A2** | Street signs, shop signs, price tags, brief messages, event invitations, store hours, classified ads. |
| **Middle items** | **B1–B2** | Personal letters, company policies, local newspaper reports, opinion pieces. |
| **Final items** | **C1–C2** | Long argumentative, academic, or literary texts. |

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--tip" markdown="1">

**The time bank strategy**  
60 minutes for 39 items gives you an average of roughly 90 seconds per item. Because the early documents are short, you can usually answer them faster than that.  
Every minute you save on the easy items is a minute you can reinvest in the demanding multi-paragraph texts at the end of the test.

### Decoding public signs pictograms and municipal notices

In public spaces, administrative buildings, transport networks, and commercial establishments, signs use concise verbal imperatives, nominal phrases, and passive constructions to communicate regulations.

#### 1. Prohibitions and negative commands (Interdictions)

Public prohibitions in France and Canada rarely use full conversational sentences (*« Vous ne devez pas... »*). Instead, they rely on nominalized warnings or the **infinitif négatif**:

| Sign Heading / Text | IPA Pronunciation | English Meaning | Regulatory Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Défense de fumer / vapoter** | /de.fɑ̃s də fy.me / va.pɔ.te/ | No smoking / vaping | Smoking or using e-cigarettes is strictly prohibited on the premises. |
| **Interdit au public / Accès interdit** | /ɛ̃.tɛʁ.di o py.blik / ak.sɛ ɛ̃.tɛʁ.di/ | Unauthorized personnel only | Only employees or authorized personnel may enter. |
| **Ne pas jeter de déchets** | /nə pa ʒə.te də de.ʃɛ/ | Do not litter | Leaving trash is subject to municipal fines. |
| **Stationnement interdit** | /sta.sjɔn.mɑ̃ ɛ̃.tɛʁ.di/ | No parking | Vehicles parked here will be towed (*remorquage*). |
| **Prière de ne pas déranger** | /pʁi.jɛʁ də nə pa de.ʁɑ̃.ʒe/ | Please do not disturb | Polite formal request to maintain privacy or quiet. |

### Reading classified ads housing and employment snippets

The early reading items often include short classified advertisements (*petites annonces*). These short snippets condense vital specifications using standardized abbreviations, symbols, and transactional formulas.

#### 1. Decoding real estate rental ads

Consider this typical classified rental snippet:

```text
À LOUER — Montréal (Plateau)
Magnifique 3½ rénové, 2e étage, très lumineux.
Cuisine équipée (frigo, poêle inclus). Chauffage et eau chaude non compris.
Buanderie dans l'immeuble. Balcon privé.
Proche métro Mont-Royal et commerces.
1 250 $ / mois. Disponible le 1er juillet.
Animaux non admis. Non-fumeur uniquement.
Contact : Mme Tremblay au 514-555-0199 (après 18 h).
```

When reading this notice, your eye must instantly isolate key criteria without translation:
- **Location**: *Montréal, quartier Plateau, proche métro*.
- **Size**: *3½* (One bedroom, living room, kitchen, bathroom in Quebec typology).
- **Inclusions / Exclusions**: *Frigo et poêle inclus* (Refrigerator and stove included); *Chauffage NON compris* (Tenant pays electric heating).
- **Restrictions**: *Animaux non admis* (No pets permitted); *Non-fumeur* (No tobacco/cannabis).
- **Date**: *Disponible le 1er juillet* (Standard moving day in Quebec).
- **Communication instructions**: Call *après 18 h* (Calling before 6:00 PM would violate instructions).

#### 2. Decoding employment and service ads

```text
OFFRE D'EMPLOI — Magasin de plein air
Recherche vendeur / vendeuse à temps partiel (20 h / semaine).
Profil : bilingue français-anglais, expérience en service à la clientèle un atout.
Disponibilité requise les vendredis soirs et les fins de semaine.
Salaire compétitif + rabais employé de 30 %.
Envoyez votre CV et lettre de motivation avant le 15 mai à : rh@pleinair-quebec.ca
```

Key scanning targets:
- **Position**: Salesperson (*vendeur/vendeuse*).
- **Hours**: Part-time (*temps partiel*), 20 hours weekly; specifically evenings and weekends.
- **Language**: Bilingual requirement (*bilingue français-anglais*).
- **Benefits**: Hourly wage + 30% employee discount (*rabais employé*).
- **Submission deadline**: May 15th via email (*courriel*).

### Skimming and scanning techniques for rapid information retrieval

To answer the early, short-document reading items accurately and quickly, execute the following **four-step tactical sequence** (the timings are suggested practice targets):

```text
Step 1: Read the Question Stem FIRST (5 seconds)
- Never read the document first! Look immediately at the question stem.
- Identify the TARGET INTERROGATIVE:
  - « Où... ? » -> Target location / room / building.
  - « Quand... ? » -> Target date / hour / season / deadline.
  - « Combien... ? » -> Target price / percentage / duration / quantity.
  - « Qui... ? » -> Target person / professional / recipient / company.
  - « Pourquoi... ? » -> Target purpose / motive / reason.

Step 2: Identify Anchor Keywords in the Stem (3 seconds)
- Highlight the 1 or 2 specific nouns or dates in the question (e.g., « 1er juillet », « animaux », « rabais »).

Step 3: Scan the Document for Visual Anchors (15–20 seconds)
- Run your eyes diagonally across the text searching ONLY for the anchor keyword or its direct synonym.
- Do NOT read every sentence. Jump directly to dollar signs ($), numbers, email addresses, or bold headers.

Step 4: Verify Context & Eliminate Distractors (15–20 seconds)
- Read the single sentence surrounding your matched keyword carefully.
- Compare with the four multiple-choice options (A, B, C, D) and eliminate false matches.
```

### Deceptive cognates and question distractor traps

Multiple-choice distractors are often built to catch candidates who skim carelessly or rely on word-for-word surface matching:

#### 1. The word-match trap (Le piège du mot identique)

If option A contains the exact identical word found in the document, **be suspicious**. Often, the document uses that word in a negative or conditional context (*« Sauf les lundis »* or *« Réservé aux membres »*), while the correct answer paraphrases the meaning using different words.
- *Document*: *« L'entrée est gratuite pour les enfants de moins de 12 ans. »*
- *Question*: *Qui ne paie pas son billet ?*
- *Trap Option*: *A) Les personnes de 12 ans et plus.* (Contains "12 ans", but inverted logic!).
- *Correct Option*: *B) Les jeunes de 10 ans.* (Paraphrase: 10 years old is under 12).

#### 2. The absolute quantifier trap

Options containing absolute words are almost always incorrect unless explicitly backed by the text:
- **Toujours** (*always*)
- **Jamais** (*never*)
- **Tous / Toutes** (*all / everyone*)
- **Uniquement / Seulement** (*only*)
- **Obligatoirement** (*mandatory*)

If an option claims *« Le magasin est ouvert tous les jours »*, but the text says *« Ouvert du lundi au samedi »*, that option is false because it ignores Sunday closure.

#### 3. Deceptive false friends (Faux amis)

| French Word in Sign | Common English Misinterpretation | Actual True Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Actuellement** | *Actually (in reality)* | **Currently / Right now** (*En ce moment*) |
| **Attendre** | *To attend (a meeting)* | **To wait for** (*Patienter*) |
| **Assister à** | *To assist (to help)* | **To attend (a concert, conference)** |
| **Avertissement** | *Advertisement* | **Warning / Notice of caution** |
| **Dépasser** | *To pass away* | **To exceed / go beyond (a speed, limit)** |
| **Monnaie** | *Money in general* | **Loose change / coins** (*Petite monnaie*) |
| **Location** | *Geographical location* | **Rental / Leasing of goods** |
