### Introduction

If there is a single grammatical hurdle that defines the transition from an elementary language learner to an independent, articulate speaker of French, it is **mastering the contrast between the passé composé and the imparfait**.

Up to this point, you have studied each tense in isolation:
- You learned that the **passé composé** reports completed past events, changes of state, and chronological actions.
- You learned that the **imparfait** paints descriptive backdrops, continuous physical/emotional states, and repeated past habits.

In real-world conversation and professional storytelling, however, these two tenses **never operate alone**. They work in constant harmony, weaving together like threads in a tapestry: the *imparfait* creates the stage, the lighting, and the background atmosphere, while the *passé composé* sends the actors across the stage to trigger events, interruptions, and plot developments.

On the **TCF Canada examination**, mastering this dual-tense interplay is the **universal discriminator between CEFR Level A2 and Level B1/B2**:
- In **Writing (*Expression écrite*) Task 2 (120–150 words)**, candidates who rely exclusively on the passé composé produce robotic, dry lists of actions. Candidates who rely exclusively on the imparfait produce static descriptions where nothing ever happens. Blending both tenses seamlessly is the exact criterion examiners evaluate under *Correction morphosyntaxique*.
- In **Listening (*Compréhension orale*)**, audio dialogues constantly rely on this contrast to convey plot twists: *« Je roulais tranquillement sur l'autoroute (imparfait) quand un camion a freiné brusquement (passé composé) »*.
- In **Speaking (*Expression orale*) Task 1 and Task 3**, when recounting an anecdote, an immigration memory, or a past obstacle, balancing background details with specific decisions marks you as an authentic, fluid speaker.

In this lesson, you will master the "Backdrop vs Event" mental model, learn the classic "Interrupted Action" structure, memorize reliable temporal trigger words, and conquer verbs that change meaning depending on the tense.

### The cinematic mental model: backdrop versus event

To eliminate confusion between the two tenses, adopt the **Cinematic Mental Model** used by linguists and professional language teachers:

```text
The Imparfait = THE SCENERY & LIGHTING (The Movie Set)
The Passé Composé = THE ACTION & PLOT TWISTS (The Script)
```

```text
IMPARFAIT (The Backdrop):
- What was the weather like? (Il faisait froid, il neigeait)
- What time was it? (Il était 18 heures)
- Where were you and how did you feel? (J'étais fatigué, j'avais faim)
- What was already in progress? (Je marchais vers la station)
              ↓
PASSÉ COMPOSÉ (The Event):
- What suddenly happened? (Soudain, j'ai entendu un bruit)
- What did you do next? (J'ai tourné la tête)
- What was the final outcome? (J'ai trouvé mon portefeuille par terre)
```

| Dimension | Imparfait (L'imparfait) | Passé Composé (Le passé composé) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Question Answered** | *What was going on? How were things?* | *What happened? What did you do?* |
| **Duration & Boundaries** | Open-ended, ongoing, no definite start or end | Closed, completed, definite start and end point |
| **Function in Narrative** | Background description, scene-setting, habits | The storyline backbone, sequential events |
| **Visual Metaphor** | A continuous video playing in the background | A camera snapshot; a point on a timeline |

Let us observe this harmony in an authentic workplace scenario:

> **Hier, il était 16 heures et je travaillais tranquillement à mon bureau (imparfait). Soudain, mon gestionnaire est entré et il m'a annoncé une excellente nouvelle (passé composé) !**  
> — /jɛʁ, i.l‿e.tɛ sɛ.z‿œʁ e ʒə tʁa.va.jɛ tʁɑ̃.kil.mɑ̃ a mɔ̃ by.ʁo. su.dɛ̃, mɔ̃ ʒɛs.tjɔ.nɛʁ ɛ.t‿ɑ̃.tʁe e i.l‿m‿a a.nɔ̃.se yn ɛk.sɛ.lɑ̃t nu.vɛl/  
> *Yesterday, it was 4:00 PM and I was working quietly at my desk (background). Suddenly, my manager came in and announced great news to me (actions)!*

Notice how *il était* and *je travaillais* set the calm environment, while *est entré* and *a annoncé* drive the story forward!

### The classic interruption formula

The most common narrative construction in French combines both tenses in a single sentence to express **an action in progress interrupted by a sudden occurrence**:

```text
[Action in progress in the IMPARFAIT] + quand / lorsque / soudain + [Interruption in the PASSÉ COMPOSÉ]
```

> **Je dormais profondément quand le réveil-matin a sonné.** — /ʒə dɔʁ.mɛ pʁɔ.fɔ̃.de.mɑ̃ kɑ̃ lə ʁe.vɛj ma.tɛ̃ a sɔ.ne/  
> *I was sleeping deeply when the alarm clock went off.*  
> (*Dormais* = ongoing background; *a sonné* = punctual interruption).

> **Nous marchions dans la rue Sainte-Catherine lorsqu'il a commencé à neiger.** — /nu maʁ.ʃjɔ̃ dɑ̃ la ʁy sɛ̃t kat.ʁin lɔʁs.k‿i.l‿a kɔ.mɑ̃.se a ne.ʒe/  
> *We were walking on Sainte-Catherine Street when it began to snow.*  
> (*Marchions* = ongoing action; *a commencé* = specific start of precipitation).

> **Pendant que je lisais mes courriels, la connexion Internet a coupé.** — /pɑ̃.dɑ̃ kə ʒə li.zɛ me ku.ʁjɛl, la kɔ.nɛk.sjɔ̃ ɛ̃.tɛʁ.nɛt a ku.pe/  
> *While I was reading my emails, the Internet connection dropped.*  
> (*Lisais* = background activity; *a coupé* = sudden disruption).

### Temporal trigger words: navigating the tense indicators

Certain adverbs and time expressions naturally signal whether the speaker is establishing a background routine or reporting a specific milestone event:

#### 1. Passé Composé Triggers (Punctual, Specific, Chronological)

These words point to specific, contained moments in time or chronological sequences:

- **un jour** (/œ̃ ʒuʁ/) — *one day*
- **soudain / tout à coup** (/su.dɛ̃ / tu.t‿a ku/) — *suddenly / all of a sudden*
- **hier / hier soir** (/jɛʁ / jɛʁ swaʁ/) — *yesterday / yesterday evening*
- **mardi dernier** (/maʁ.di dɛʁ.nje/) — *last Tuesday*
- **à ce moment-là** (/a sə mɔ.mɑ̃ la/) — *at that exact moment*
- **d'abord, ensuite, puis, enfin** (/d‿a.bɔʁ, ɑ̃.sɥit, pɥi, ɑ̃.fɛ̃/) — *first, then, afterward, finally*
- **pendant deux heures** (/pɑ̃.dɑ̃ dø.z‿œʁ/) — *for two hours (completed duration)*

#### 2. Imparfait Triggers (Habitual, Continuous, Descriptive)

These words indicate recurring actions, ongoing states, or indefinite timeframes:

- **autrefois / à l'époque** (/o.tʁə.fwa / a l‿e.pɔk/) — *formerly / back then*
- **d'habitude / habituellement** (/d‿a.bi.tyd / a.bi.tɥɛl.mɑ̃/) — *usually / habitually*
- **toujours** (/tu.ʒuʁ/) — *always (habitual)*
- **souvent** (/su.vɑ̃/) — *often*
- **tous les jours / chaque matin** (/tu le ʒuʁ / ʃak ma.tɛ̃/) — *every day / every morning*
- **le lundi** (/lə lœ̃.di/) — *on Mondays (repetitive habit)*
- **pendant que** (/pɑ̃.dɑ̃ kə/) — *while (action in progress)*

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--critical" markdown="1">

#### Critical Nuance: "Pendant" with Passé Composé vs Imparfait

Take note of the difference between **pendant** and **pendant que**:
- **pendant que + clause** takes the **imparfait** because it describes an action in progress:  
  *Pendant que j'attendais le bus...* (*While I was waiting for the bus...*)
- **pendant + completed time limit** takes the **passé composé** because the duration is closed and finished:  
  *J'ai attendu le bus pendant vingt minutes.* (*I waited for the bus for 20 minutes.*)

</div>

### Verbs that shift meaning between tenses

Certain common French verbs express fundamentally different concepts depending on whether they are conjugated in the imparfait or the passé composé. Understanding these shifts is essential for precision on the TCF:

| Verb | Meaning in the IMPARFAIT (State / Condition) | Meaning in the PASSÉ COMPOSÉ (Action / Result) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **savoir** | **knew** (held knowledge over time)<br>*Je savais la vérité.* (I knew the truth.) | **found out / learned** (moment of discovery)<br>*J'ai su la nouvelle hier.* (I found out yesterday.) |
| **connaître**| **knew / was familiar with**<br>*Je connaissais ce quartier.* (I knew this area.) | **met for the first time**<br>*J'ai connu mon mari à Québec.* (I met my husband in QC.) |
| **vouloir** | **wanted / wished** (ongoing mental desire)<br>*Je voulais partir tôt.* (I wanted to leave early.) | **tried / decided / refused (with negative)**<br>*J'ai voulu ouvrir la porte.* (I tried to open the door.)<br>*Il n'a pas voulu signer.* (He refused to sign.) |
| **pouvoir** | **could / was capable of** (general ability)<br>*Je pouvais courir vite.* (I could run fast.) | **managed to / succeeded in** (specific outcome)<br>*J'ai pu obtenir le visa.* (I managed to get the visa.) |
| **avoir** | **had** (ongoing state)<br>*J'avais un appartement.* (I had an apartment.) | **received / got** (event)<br>*J'ai eu un appel de la banque.* (I got a call from the bank.) |

> **Compare**:  
> *Je savais parler français.* (I knew how to speak French — ongoing ability).  
> *Quand j'ai vu l'affiche, j'ai su que c'était pour moi.* (When I saw the poster, I realized / found out it was for me — moment of realization).

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--tip" markdown="1">

#### Québec vs France: Spoken Narration

In informal Québec speech, you will frequently hear speakers punctuate past anecdotes using the present tense (*le présent de narration*) to create dramatic immediacy:
> *« Là, j'étais dans mon char (imparfait), pis tout d'un coup un chevreuil traverse la route (présent) ! »* **(QC)**

While highly authentic in informal Canadian conversation, **on the TCF Canada exam**, France Éducation international examiners expect candidates to demonstrate mastery of standard past concordances:
- *« Je conduisais ma voiture (imparfait) quand soudain un cerf a traversé la route (passé composé). »*

Adhering strictly to the imparfait / passé composé balance in your writing ensures top marks under *Correction grammaticale*.

</div>

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--warning" markdown="1">

#### Exam tip: The TCF Writing Task 2 Scoring Blueprint

On **TCF Writing Task 2 (120–150 words)**, you are required to write a personal recount or article (e.g. *« Racontez un événement marquant de votre vie professionnelle ou personnelle »*).

To achieve a **Level B1 or B2 score**, structure your narrative using the **60/40 Rule**:
1. **40% Imparfait (The Backdrop)**: In your opening paragraph, describe the time, season, location, and your emotional or professional state at the time (*« C'était en 2022. J'habitais encore dans mon pays natal et je travaillais dans une petite entreprise. J'étais très motivé... »*).
2. **60% Passé Composé (The Catalyst & Plot)**: Introduce the turning point with a strong connector (*« Un jour, j'ai reçu... J'ai décidé de... Nous avons préparé... Finalement, je suis arrivé à Montréal... »*).

This dual-layer structure guarantees variety, satisfies the FEI narrative coherence rubric, and eliminates monotonous syntax!
