### Introduction

In everyday life and professional decision-making, you are constantly called upon to weigh options: Is living in Montreal cheaper than living in Toronto? Which apartment has a better location? Which job offer provides more benefits or a more flexible telework schedule?

In French, evaluating differences and making choices requires mastering **comparatives and superlatives** (*les comparatifs et les superlatifs*).

On the **TCF Canada examination**, comparison structures are a mandatory requirement across all four evaluation modules:
- In **Writing (*Expression écrite*) Task 2 (120–150 words)**, candidates are routinely asked to compare two situations: comparing living in the city versus the country, working from home versus going to the office, or comparing a previous job with a current position. Using precise comparative formulas (*plus... que, moins... que, autant de... que*) is essential for achieving a B1/B2 score under *Cohérence et cohésion*.
- In **Speaking (*Expression orale*) Task 3 (unscripted opinion monologue)**, the examiner will prompt you to analyze alternatives: *« Pensez-vous qu'il est préférable de louer ou d'acheter son logement ? »* (*Do you think it is preferable to rent or buy one's home?*).
- In **Reading (*Compréhension écrite*)**, consumer product reviews, economic reports comparing provincial tax rates, and classified real-estate summaries test your ability to determine which option is the most economical or convenient.

In this lesson, you will master the distinct formulas for comparing adjectives, adverbs, nouns, and verbs, conquer the notorious **meilleur versus mieux** trap, learn to construct superlatives, and practice comparing career and lifestyle options in Canada.

### Lesson overview

This section contains a general overview of topics that you will learn in this lesson.

- The three degrees of comparison: Superiority (+), Inferiority (-), and Equality (=).
- Comparing qualities with adjectives and adverbs: **plus... que**, **moins... que**, and **aussi... que**.
- Comparing quantities with nouns: **plus de... que**, **moins de... que**, and **autant de... que**.
- Comparing actions with verbs: **verbe + plus que**, **moins que**, and **autant que**.
- Irregular comparatives: the critical difference between the adjective **meilleur** and the adverb **mieux**.
- Expressing the extreme with superlatives: **le plus**, **la plus**, **les plus**, **le moins**, and **le meilleur**.
- Comparing Canadian cities, housing costs, and workplace benefits for TCF Writing Task 2.

### Comparing adjectives and adverbs: qualities

When comparing the qualities or characteristics of two people, places, or objects, use an adjective or an adverb surrounded by the comparative bracket:

```text
Superiority (+): [plus] + Adjective / Adverb + [que / qu']
Inferiority   (-): [moins] + Adjective / Adverb + [que / qu']
Equality      (=): [aussi] + Adjective / Adverb + [que / qu']
```

Remember: **adjectives must always agree in gender and number with the subject they modify!**

| Comparative Type | French Structure | IPA Pronunciation | English Meaning | Example in Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Superiority (+)** | **plus** grand **que** | /ply ɡʁɑ̃ kə/ | bigger than | *Ce 4½ est **plus grand que** mon ancien logement.* |
| **Inferiority (-)** | **moins** cher **que** | /mwɛ̃ ʃɛʁ kə/ | less expensive than | *Le loyer à Sherbrooke est **moins cher qu'**à Montréal.* |
| **Equality (=)** | **aussi** lumineux **que**| /o.si ly.mi.nø kə/ | as bright as | *Le salon est **aussi lumineux que** la terrasse.* |
| **Adverb (+)** | **plus** rapidement **que**| /ply ʁa.pid.mɑ̃ kə/ | more quickly than | *Le métro circule **plus rapidement que** l'autobus.* |
| **Adverb (=)** | **aussi** bien **que** | /o.si bjɛ̃ kə/ | as well as | *Elle parle français **aussi bien que** son collègue.* |

Notice that before a vowel sound, **que** elides to **qu'**:
> *Montréal est plus abordable **qu'**Ottawa.* (/mɔ̃.ʁe.al ɛ ply.z‿a.bɔʁ.dabl k‿ɔ.ta.wa/)

### Comparing nouns: quantities

When comparing the quantity or amount of things (tangible goods, money, hours, or abstract concepts), the formula changes in two crucial ways:
1. You must insert the preposition **de** (or **d'** before a vowel) directly after the comparative word.
2. For equality, **aussi** changes to **autant de**!

```text
Superiority (+): [plus de] + Noun + [que / qu']
Inferiority   (-): [moins de] + Noun + [que / qu']
Equality      (=): [autant de] + Noun + [que / qu']
```

| Comparative Type | French Structure | IPA | English Meaning | Example in Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Superiority (+)** | **plus de** travail **que** | /ply də tʁa.vaj kə/ | more work than | *J'ai **plus de travail que** la semaine dernière.* |
| **Inferiority (-)** | **moins de** bruit **que** | /mwɛ̃ də bʁɥi kə/ | less noise than | *Il y a **moins de bruit que** sur l'autoroute.* |
| **Equality (=)** | **autant de** congés **que** | /o.tɑ̃ də kɔ̃.ʒe kə/ | as many holidays as | *Nous avons **autant de congés que** nos collègues.* |
| **Vowel Elision** | **plus d'**espace **que** | /ply d‿ɛs.pas kə/ | more space than | *Ce logement a **plus d'espace que** le studio.* |

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--critical" markdown="1">

#### Critical Grammar Trap: "Aussi" vs "Autant"

English uses the word *"as"* for both qualities and quantities (*as tall as* vs *as much money as*).

In French, you must never use *aussi* with nouns:
- **Incorrect**: *"J'ai aussi d'argent que toi."* (Severe error)
- **Correct**: **J'ai autant d'argent que toi.** (/ʒ‿e o.tɑ̃ d‿aʁ.ʒɑ̃ kə twa/ — *I have as much money as you.*)

Remember the rule: **Aussi** goes with Adjectives and Adverbs; **Autant de** goes with Nouns!

</div>

### Comparing verbs: actions

When comparing the intensity, frequency, or duration of an action, the comparative word follows the verb directly:

```text
Superiority (+): [Conjugated Verb] + [plus que]
Inferiority   (-): [Conjugated Verb] + [moins que]
Equality      (=): [Conjugated Verb] + [autant que]
```

In comparisons with verbs, the final **-s** of **plus** is **pronounced** as **/plys/** to clearly distinguish it from negative *ne... plus*:

> **Je travaille plus que mon collègue.** — /ʒə tʁa.vaj plys kə mɔ̃ kɔ.lɛɡ/  
> *I work more than my colleague.*

> **En télétravail, je dépense moins qu'au bureau.** — /ɑ̃ te.le.tʁa.vaj, ʒə de.pɑ̃s mwɛ̃ k‿o by.ʁo/  
> *In telework, I spend less than at the office.*

> **Il gagne autant que son superviseur.** — /il ɡaɲ o.tɑ̃ kə sɔ̃ sy.pɛʁ.vi.zœʁ/  
> *He earns as much as his supervisor.*

### Irregular comparatives: the meilleur versus mieux trap

The single most common comparative error made by foreign language learners on the TCF Canada is confusing **meilleur** and **mieux**.

French does not permit saying *"plus bon"* or *"plus bien"*. Instead, you must use their dedicated irregular comparative forms:

```text
bon (good - adjective) → MEILLEUR (better - adjective)
bien (well - adverb)   → MIEUX (better - adverb)
```

| Base Word | Type | Irregular Comparative | Agreement Rule | Example in Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **bon** (good) | Adjective | **meilleur** (/mɛ.jœʁ/) | Agrees with noun (*-e, -s, -es*) | *Cet appartement est **meilleur** que l'autre.*<br>*Cette offre est **meilleure**.* |
| **bien** (well) | Adverb | **mieux** (/mjø/) | Invariable (never changes) | *Je me sens **mieux** aujourd'hui.*<br>*Il travaille **mieux** en équipe.* |
| **mauvais** (bad)| Adjective | **pire** (/piʁ/) or *plus mauvais* | Agrees in number (*-s*) | *La circulation est **pire** le vendredi.* |

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--critical" markdown="1">

#### The 5-Second Test: "Meilleur" or "Mieux"?

To determine whether to write *meilleur* or *mieux*:
1. Ask yourself: am I modifying a **noun** (a thing or person) or a **verb** (an action)?
   - If modifying a **noun**, use **meilleur(e)**:  
     *Ce quartier est **meilleur**.* (*Le quartier* is a noun).  
     *La qualité de vie est **meilleure**.* (*La qualité* is a feminine noun).
   - If modifying a **verb**, use **mieux**:  
     *Ce quartier est **mieux** desservi par le métro.* (*Desservi* is a verbal past participle; *mieux* describes how it is served).  
     *Je comprends **mieux** le français maintenant.* (*Comprends* is a verb).

Never say *"C'est plus bon"* or *"Il conduit meilleur"*!

</div>

### Superlatives: expressing the extreme

The **superlative** expresses the highest or lowest degree of a quality within a group (*the biggest, the least expensive, the best*).

To form the superlative of an adjective, place the appropriate definite article (**le, la, les**) in front of the comparative word:

```text
[le / la / les] + [plus / moins] + Adjective + [de + category]
```

> **Montréal est la plus grande ville de la province de Québec.** — /mɔ̃.ʁe.al ɛ la ply ɡʁɑ̃d vil də la pʁɔ.vɛ̃s də ke.bɛk/  
> *Montreal is the biggest city in the province of Quebec.*

> **C'est le logement le moins cher du quartier.** — /s‿ɛ lə lɔʒ.mɑ̃ lə mwɛ̃ ʃɛʁ dy kaʁ.tje/  
> *It is the least expensive dwelling in the neighborhood.*

> **Le meilleur candidat obtiendra le poste permanent.** — /lə mɛ.jœʁ kɑ̃.di.da ɔb.tjɛ̃.dʁa lə pɔst pɛʁ.ma.nɑ̃/  
> *The best candidate will obtain the permanent position.*

Notice that in French, the preposition following a superlative is **de** (*in / of*), not *dans*:
- *La plus belle ville **du** monde* (not *"dans le monde"*).
- *Le meilleur employé **de** l'entreprise* (not *"dans l'entreprise"*).

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--tip" markdown="1">

#### Québec vs France

In Québec colloquial French, you will encounter colorful expressions using comparatives:
- **Pas pire** **(QC)** (/pa piʁ/): Literally meaning "not worse", this is ubiquitous Canadian slang for *"pretty good"*, *"not bad"*, or *"decent"*:
  > *« Comment tu trouves ton nouvel appart ? — Pas pire pantoute ! »* **(QC)**  
  > (Meaning: *"How do you like your new apartment? — Pretty good, not bad at all!"*)
- **Bien pire** (/bjɛ̃ piʁ/): Widely used to emphasize that a situation is far worse:
  > *« L'hiver à Winnipeg est bien pire qu'à Vancouver ! »*

On the TCF Canada exam, use standard comparatives (*très satisfaisant, bien supérieur*) in your writing, but recognizing *pas pire* will help your ear on informal Canadian listening dialogues!

</div>

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--warning" markdown="1">

#### Exam tip: TCF Writing Task 2 Comparison Blueprint

On **TCF Writing Task 2 (120–150 words)**, comparison prompts are among the most frequent (e.g. *« Vous écrivez à un ami pour comparer votre vie dans votre pays d'origine et votre nouvelle vie au Canada »*).

To score at Level B2:
1. **Compare Qualities (Adjectives)**: *« Le rythme de vie ici est plus calme et l'air est plus pur qu'auparavant. »*
2. **Compare Quantities (Nouns)**: *« Même si j'ai moins de temps libre, j'ai plus d'opportunités professionnelles. »*
3. **Use Irregular Forms**: *« Le système de transport en commun est bien meilleur et les services publics fonctionnent mieux. »*
4. **Conclude with a Superlative**: *« C'est la meilleure décision que j'ai prise pour l'avenir de ma famille. »*

Demonstrating this complete grammatical spectrum in a single 130-word text fulfills the highest tier of the FEI lexical and morphosyntactic criteria!

</div>
