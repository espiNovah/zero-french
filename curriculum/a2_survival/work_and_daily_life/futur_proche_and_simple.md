### Introduction

After mastering past narration with the *passé composé* and the *imparfait*, your linguistic toolkit needs the final temporal dimension: **the future**. Whether describing your upcoming vacation, outlining your career ambitions in Canada, predicting weather changes, or reading company project forecasts, talking about what will happen is a daily necessity.

In French, the future is expressed through two primary grammatical structures:
1. **The Futur Proche (Immediate Future)**: Formed with **aller + infinitive** (*Je vais déménager*). This is the conversational workhorse used in spoken French for planned, imminent, or high-certainty events.
2. **The Futur Simple (Simple Future)**: A single-word synthetic tense (*Je déménagerai*). This is the formal, projected, and official future tense used in written reports, employment contracts, long-term career planning, and weather forecasts.

On the **TCF Canada examination**, future structures are tested across all four competencies:
- In **Speaking (*Expression orale*) Task 1**, following your introduction and current work duties, examiners frequently ask: *« Quels sont vos projets d'avenir au Canada ? »* (*What are your future projects in Canada?*). Balancing *futur proche* for immediate steps with *futur simple* for long-term career goals immediately signals Upper A2 / B1 proficiency.
- In **Writing (*Expression écrite*) Task 1 and Task 2**, prompts regularly ask you to propose an upcoming event, confirm an arrival schedule, or write about future plans.
- In **Listening (*Compréhension orale*)**, distinguishing between past actions (*j'ai fait*), present states (*je fais*), and future actions (*je ferai* / *je vais faire*) is a classic test design technique used to create multiple-choice distractors.

In this lesson, you will master the mechanics of both future tenses, learn regular and irregular future stems, understand when to choose one over the other, and practice projecting your career in Canada.

### The conversational future: futur proche mechanics

You were introduced to the **futur proche** in A1. Because it is simple to form and expresses immediate, concrete certainty, it accounts for the vast majority of future statements in everyday Francophone conversation.

The formula consists of the present tense of **aller** followed directly by an **infinitive**:

```text
[Subject] + [Aller in Present Tense] + [Infinitive Verb]
```

| Subject Pronoun | Aller Conjugation | IPA | Example with *travailler* | English Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Je** | **vais** | /vɛ/ | **Je vais travailler** | I am going to work |
| **Tu** | **vas** | /va/ | **Tu vas travailler** | you are going to work |
| **Il / Elle / On** | **va** | /va/ | **Il va travailler** | he / she is going to work |
| **Nous** | **allons** | /a.lɔ̃/ | **Nous allons travailler** | we are going to work |
| **Vous** | **allez** | /a.le/ | **Vous allez travailler** | you are going to work |
| **Ils / Elles** | **vont** | /vɔ̃/ | **Ils vont travailler** | they are going to work |

#### Negation and Pronoun Placement with Futur Proche

- **Negation**: *Ne ... pas* wraps around the conjugated auxiliary verb **aller**:
  > **Je ne vais pas accepter ce poste.** — /ʒə nə vɛ pa.z‿ak.sɛp.te sə pɔst/  
  > *I am not going to accept this position.*
- **Object Pronoun Placement**: The pronoun sits immediately before the **infinitive verb**:
  > **Je vais lui téléphoner.** — /ʒə vɛ lɥi te.le.fɔ.ne/  
  > *I am going to call him/her.*

### The formal future: futur simple mechanics and endings

The **futur simple** is not formed with an auxiliary verb; it is a **single-word conjugated tense**.

Historically, the futur simple was created by attaching the present tense forms of *avoir* to the infinitive of the verb (*parler + ai = parlerai*). This historic origin gives us the **universal future endings**, which are identical for every single verb in the French language:

```text
Universal Future Endings:
-ai  ·  -as  ·  -a  ·  -ons  ·  -ez  ·  -ont
```

#### 1. Regular Verb Stems

For regular **-er** and **-ir** verbs, the stem is simply the **entire infinitive**:

- **parler** → stem **parler-** → *je parlerai, tu parleras, il parlera, nous parlerons, vous parlerez, ils parleront*
- **finir** → stem **finir-** → *je finirai, tu finiras, il finira, nous finirons, vous finirez, ils finiront*

For regular **-re** verbs, drop the silent final **-e** before adding the endings so the stem ends in **-r**:
- **attendre** → stem **attendr-** → *j'attendrai, tu attendras, il attendra, nous attendrons, vous attendrez, ils attendront*
- **vendre** → stem **vendr-** → *je vendrai, tu vendras, il vendra, nous vendrons, vous vendrez, ils vendront*

| Subject Pronoun | Future Ending | IPA | Example with *réussir* | Example with *attendre* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Je** | **-ai** | /e/ | **Je réussirai** (/ʁe.y.si.ʁe/) | **J'attendrai** (/ʒ‿a.tɑ̃.dʁe/) |
| **Tu** | **-as** | /a/ | **Tu réussiras** (/ty ʁe.y.si.ʁa/) | **Tu attendras** (/ty a.tɑ̃.dʁa/) |
| **Il / Elle / On** | **-a** | /a/ | **Il réussira** (/il ʁe.y.si.ʁa/) | **Il attendra** (/i.l‿a.tɑ̃.dʁa/) |
| **Nous** | **-ons** | /ɔ̃/ | **Nous réussirons** (/nu ʁe.y.si.ʁɔ̃/) | **Nous attendrons** (/nu.z‿a.tɑ̃.dʁɔ̃/) |
| **Vous** | **-ez** | /e/ | **Vous réussirez** (/vu ʁe.y.si.ʁe/) | **Vous attendrez** (/vu.z‿a.tɑ̃.dʁe/) |
| **Ils / Elles** | **-ont** | /ɔ̃/ | **Ils réussiront** (/il ʁe.y.si.ʁɔ̃/) | **Ils attendront** (/il.z‿a.tɑ̃.dʁɔ̃/) |

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--critical" markdown="1">

#### Phonetic Trap: "Je parlerai" (Future) vs "Je parlais" (Imparfait)

Listen carefully to the final vowel sound:
- **Futur simple**: *Je parlerai* ends in a closed **/e/** (like the *é* in *café*).
- **Imparfait**: *Je parlais* ends in an open **/ɛ/** (like the *è* in *très* or English *bed*).

Mixing up these two sounds can lead to severe confusion in TCF Listening between past habits and future commitments!

</div>

### High-frequency irregular future stems

While the endings of the futur simple are 100% regular, many of the most essential verbs in the language possess **irregular stems**. However, every single future stem in French **ends in the letter -r** (or **-rr**):

| Infinitive Verb | Irregular Future Stem | 1st Person Form & IPA | English Meaning | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **être** | **ser-** | **Je serai** (/ʒə sə.ʁe/) | I will be | *Je serai disponible demain matin.* |
| **avoir** | **aur-** | **J'aurai** (/ʒ‿o.ʁe/) | I will have | *J'aurai les résultats du test la semaine prochaine.* |
| **faire** | **fer-** | **Je ferai** (/ʒə fə.ʁe/) | I will do / make | *Nous ferons une demande de résidence permanente.* |
| **aller** | **ir-** | **J'irai** (/ʒ‿i.ʁe/) | I will go | *J'irai à l'agence d'immigration jeudi.* |
| **pouvoir** | **pourr-** (double r) | **Je pourrai** (/ʒə pu.ʁe/) | I will be able to | *Vous pourrez signer le contrat dès lundi.* |
| **vouloir** | **voudr-** | **Je voudrai** (/ʒə vu.dʁe/) | I will want | *Nous voudrons visiter le quartier.* |
| **savoir** | **saur-** | **Je saurai** (/ʒə so.ʁe/) | I will know | *Je saurai ma note bientôt.* |
| **devoir** | **devr-** | **Je devrai** (/ʒə də.vʁe/) | I will have to | *Il devra renouveler son permis de travail.* |
| **venir** | **viendr-** | **Je viendrai** (/ʒə vjɛ̃.dʁe/) | I will come | *Mon ami viendra me voir à Montréal.* |
| **envoyer** | **enverr-** (double r) | **J'enverrai** (/ʒ‿ɑ̃.vɛ.ʁe/) | I will send | *J'enverrai mon CV par courriel ce soir.* |
| **voir** | **verr-** (double r) | **Je verrai** (/ʒə vɛ.ʁe/) | I will see | *Nous verrons les options d'hébergement.* |

> **Il y aura** (/il j‿o.ʁa/) is the future form of *il y a* (*there will be*):  
> *Il y aura une réunion demain à 9 heures.* (There will be a meeting tomorrow at 9:00 AM).

### Deciding between futur proche and futur simple

When should you use the *futur proche*, and when should you choose the *futur simple*?

```text
FUTUR PROCHE (Aller + Infinitif):
- Near future, immediate actions (dans 5 minutes, ce soir, demain)
- Spoken conversations, informal planning
- Direct cause-and-effect visible right now (Regarde les nuages, il va pleuvoir !)

FUTUR SIMPLE (Infinitive Stem + Endings):
- Distant future, career milestones, life goals (dans 5 ans, à l'avenir)
- Formal written correspondence, official memos, contracts, laws
- Weather broadcasts (Demain, les températures chuteront sous zéro)
- Conditional hypotheses with "Si" (Si j'obtiens le TCF, j'immigrerai au Canada)
```

| Situation | Preferred Tense | French Example | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Immediate plan today** | *Futur proche* | *Je **vais préparer** mes valises ce soir.* | I'm going to pack my bags tonight. |
| **Long-term career goal** | *Futur simple* | *Dans cinq ans, je **dirigerai** un service.* | In five years, I will lead a department. |
| **Formal email notice** | *Futur simple* | *La direction vous **transmettra** le rapport.* | Management will forward you the report. |
| **Casual conversation** | *Futur proche* | *On **va manger** ensemble à midi ?* | Are we going to eat together at noon? |

### Temporal markers for the future

To situate future plans on a timeline, use these essential temporal connectors:

- **demain / après-demain** (/də.mɛ̃ / a.pʁɛ də.mɛ̃/) — *tomorrow / the day after tomorrow*
- **bientôt** (/bjɛ̃.to/) — *soon*
- **prochain / prochaine** (/pʁɔ.ʃɛ̃ / pʁɔ.ʃɛn/) — *next (le mois prochain, la semaine prochaine)*
- **dans + duration** (/dɑ̃/) — *in [amount of time]* (*dans deux semaines, dans six mois*)
- **à l'avenir** (/a l‿av.niʁ/) — *in the future*
- **plus tard** (/ply taʁ/) — *later / in the future*

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--tip" markdown="1">

#### Québec vs France

In spoken Canadian French, the *futur proche* is used even more extensively than in France. In everyday informal speech across Québec, speakers frequently shorten *je vais* to **m'as** **(QC)**:
> *« M'as faire ça tout de suite ! »* **(QC)**  
> (Standard French: *« Je vais faire ça tout de suite ! »*)

On the **TCF Canada examination**, always produce standard French (*je vais faire...* or *je ferai...*). However, in **TCF Listening**, being aware of rapid Canadian cadences ensures you recognize immediate future intentions without hesitation!

</div>

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--warning" markdown="1">

#### Exam tip: TCF Speaking Task 1 Career Projections

In **TCF Speaking Task 1 (self-introduction and background)**, examiners almost universally conclude by asking: *« Où vous voyez-vous dans trois ou cinq ans au Canada ? »*.

To demonstrate B1/B2 versatility:
1. Use the **futur proche** for immediate steps over the next few months:  
   *« Dans les prochains mois, je **vais passer** le test de français et je **vais finaliser** ma demande d'immigration. »*
2. Shift to the **futur simple** for your long-term professional aspirations:  
   *« Une fois installé au Canada, j'**exercerai** mon métier d'ingénieur, j'**intégrerai** une entreprise dynamique et ma famille **s'établira** durablement dans la région de Montréal. »*

This deliberate contrast between immediate steps (*vais passer*) and formal aspirations (*exercerai, intégrerai*) exhibits sophisticated temporal control!
