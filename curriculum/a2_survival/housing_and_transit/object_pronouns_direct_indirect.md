### Introduction

In everyday French conversation, repeating full noun phrases over and over makes speech tedious and unnatural. Consider how awkward it sounds to say: *« J'ai vu l'appartement hier. J'ai aimé l'appartement. Ensuite, j'ai téléphoné au propriétaire pour dire au propriétaire que je voulais louer l'appartement »*.

To speak fluently, concisely, and naturally, you must replace repeated nouns with **object pronouns** (*les pronoms compléments*). Instead of repeating *l'appartement* three times, a French speaker says: *« Je **l'**ai aimé »* (*I liked **it***) and *« Je **lui** ai téléphoné »* (*I phoned **him***).

On the **TCF Canada examination**, object pronouns are an explicit marker of **A2/B1 proficiency**:
- In **Writing (*Expression écrite*) Tasks 1, 2, and 3**, the France Éducation international (FEI) grading rubric evaluates *Étendue et maîtrise du lexique* and *Cohérence et cohésion*. Replacing nouns with direct and indirect pronouns demonstrates text cohesion and prevents monotonous repetition.
- In **Speaking (*Expression orale*) Task 2 (the formal inquiry roleplay)**, when asking a landlord or employer questions, using pronouns is essential: *« Est-ce que vous pouvez **me** montrer le logement ? »* (*Can you show **me** the accommodation?*) or *« Je **vous** remercie »* (*I thank **you***).
- In **Listening (*Compréhension orale*)**, recognizing who is receiving the action (*« Elle **lui** a envoyé les clés »* vs *« Elle **les** a envoyées »*) is critical for answering who did what in administrative and workplace dialogues.

In this lesson, you will master the distinction between **Direct Object Pronouns (COD)** and **Indirect Object Pronouns (COI)**, learn where to place them in present, past, and modal sentences, conquer the universal *lui/leur* rule, and eliminate awkward noun repetitions.

### Lesson overview

This section contains a general overview of topics that you will learn in this lesson.

- The fundamental grammatical distinction: Direct Objects (answering *qui ?* or *quoi ?*) versus Indirect Objects (answering *à qui ?*).
- The direct object pronouns (**COD**): **me**, **te**, **le / la / l'**, **nous**, **vous**, and **les**.
- The indirect object pronouns (**COI**): **me**, **te**, **lui**, **nous**, **vous**, and **leur**.
- The non-gendered nature of indirect pronouns: why **lui** means both "to him" and "to her".
- Pronoun placement rules: before simple conjugated verbs, in compound past tenses (*passé composé*), and with semi-auxiliaries / modal verbs (*aller, vouloir, pouvoir, devoir*).
- Negative sentence syntax: wrapping *ne ... pas* around the pronoun and verb combination.
- Practical communicative application in housing transactions and daily service interactions.

### Direct object pronouns: replacing people and things

A **Direct Object** (*Complément d'Objet Direct* or **COD**) is a noun that directly receives the action of the verb without any intervening preposition. It answers the question **qui ?** (*who?*) or **quoi ?** (*what?*):

> **J'attends l'autobus.** (*What do I wait for?* → *L'autobus* = Direct Object).  
> **Je connais le propriétaire.** (*Who do I know?* → *Le propriétaire* = Direct Object).

The French direct object pronouns correspond to English *me, you, him, her, it, us,* and *them*:

| Person | Pronoun | IPA | English Meaning | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st sg.** | **me / m'** | /mə / m/ | me | *Le propriétaire **m'**attend à midi.* |
| **2nd sg.** | **te / t'** | /tə / t/ | you (informal) | *Je **t'**appelle dès que j'arrive.* |
| **3rd sg. masc.** | **le / l'** | /lə / l/ | him / it (masc.) | *Cet appartement est beau, je **le** prends.* |
| **3rd sg. fem.** | **la / l'** | /la / l/ | her / it (fem.) | *Cette annonce est intéressante, je **la** lis.* |
| **1st pl.** | **nous** | /nu/ | us | *L'agent immobilier **nous** accompagne.* |
| **2nd pl.** | **vous** | /vu/ | you (formal / plural) | *Je **vous** écoute avec attention.* |
| **3rd pl.** | **les** | /le/ | them (masc. & fem.) | *Les clés ? Je **les** ai sur moi.* |

Notice that before a verb beginning with a vowel or silent *h*, **me, te, le,** and **la** elide to **m', t',** and **l'**:
- *Je regarde l'appartement.* → *Je **l'**aime beaucoup.* (/ʒə l‿ɛm bo.ku/)
- *Il écoute la radio.* → *Il **l'**écoute tous les soirs.* (/i.l‿l‿e.kut tu le swaʁ/)

### Indirect object pronouns: replacing people introduced by the preposition a

An **Indirect Object** (*Complément d'Objet Indirect* or **COI**) refers to a **person** introduced by the preposition **à** (or its contractions *au, à la, à l', aux*). It answers the question **à qui ?** (*to whom?*):

> **Je parle au propriétaire.** (*To whom do I speak?* → *Au propriétaire* = Indirect Object).  
> **Elle écrit à son amie.** (*To whom does she write?* → *À son amie* = Indirect Object).

The French indirect object pronouns are:

| Person | Pronoun | IPA | English Meaning | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st sg.** | **me / m'** | /mə / m/ | to me | *Il **me** donne les clés.* |
| **2nd sg.** | **te / t'** | /tə / t/ | to you (informal) | *Je **te** réponds immédiatement.* |
| **3rd sg. (m. & f.)**| **lui** | /lɥi/ | **to him / to her** | *Je **lui** pose une question.* |
| **1st pl.** | **nous** | /nu/ | to us | *Le concierge **nous** explique le règlement.*|
| **2nd pl.** | **vous** | /vu/ | to you (formal / pl.) | *Je **vous** envoie le bail par courriel.* |
| **3rd pl. (m. & f.)**| **leur** | /lœʁ/ | **to them** | *Nous **leur** montrons le quartier.* |

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--critical" markdown="1">

#### Critical Grammar Trap: "Lui" and "Leur" Have No Gender!

English speakers frequently make the error of trying to find a feminine form for *lui*. In French:
- **Lui** replaces **à + any singular person**, whether male or female!
  > *Je parle à Marc.* → **Je lui parle.** (*I speak to him.*)  
  > *Je parle à Sophie.* → **Je lui parle.** (*I speak to her.*)  
  > *(Never say "Je la parle" or "Je à elle parle"!)*
- **Leur** replaces **à + any plural persons**, whether masculine, feminine, or mixed!
  > *J'écris aux propriétaires.* → **Je leur écris.** (*I write to them.*)  
  > *J'écris aux colocataires (f.).* → **Je leur écris.** (*I write to them.*)  
  > *(Notice that "leur" as an object pronoun never takes an "-s"!).*

</div>

#### High-Frequency Verbs That Take an Indirect Object (Verbs with À)

To use *lui* and *leur* correctly, you must know which common verbs require the preposition **à**:

- **téléphoner à** (to call / phone someone): *Je téléphone à Marie → Je **lui** téléphone.*
- **parler à** (to speak to someone): *Nous parlons aux voisins → Nous **leur** parlons.*
- **écrire à** (to write to someone): *J'écris au propriétaire → Je **lui** écris.*
- **demander à** (to ask someone): *Elle demande à l'agent → Elle **lui** demande.*
- **répondre à** (to answer someone): *Il répond à sa mère → Il **lui** répond.*
- **donner / envoyer à** (to give / send to someone): *J'envoie le chèque au gérant → Je **lui** envoie le chèque.*
- **expliquer à** (to explain to someone): *Le médecin explique au patient → Le médecin **lui** explique.*

### Positioning rules: present, past, and modal structures

Unlike English, where object pronouns follow the verb (*"I see him"*, *"I talk to them"*), in French, **the object pronoun is placed immediately BEFORE the conjugated verb**.

Let us master pronoun placement across the three primary sentence structures tested on the TCF Canada:

#### 1. In Simple Tenses (Present and Imparfait)

The pronoun sits directly between the subject pronoun and the conjugated verb:

```text
[Subject] + [PRONOUN] + [Conjugated Verb]
```

> **Je connais ce quartier. → Je le connais très bien.** — /ʒə lə kɔ.nɛ tʁɛ bjɛ̃/  
> *I know this neighborhood. → I know it very well.*

> **Le propriétaire répond aux locataires. → Le propriétaire leur répond.** — /lə pʁɔ.pʁi.je.tɛʁ lœʁ ʁe.pɔ̃/  
> *The landlord replies to the tenants. → The landlord replies to them.*

In negative sentences, **ne ... pas wraps around both the pronoun and the verb**:

```text
[Subject] + ne + [PRONOUN] + [Verb] + pas
```

> **Je ne le connais pas.** — /ʒə nə lə kɔ.nɛ pa/ — *I do not know him / it.*  
> **Nous ne lui parlons pas.** — /nu nə lɥi paʁ.lɔ̃ pa/ — *We do not speak to him / her.*

#### 2. In Compound Tenses (Passé Composé)

In compound tenses, the pronoun sits **immediately before the auxiliary verb** (*avoir* or *être*), not before the past participle:

```text
[Subject] + [PRONOUN] + [Auxiliary Avoir/Être] + [Past Participle]
```

> **J'ai visité cet appartement. → Je l'ai visité hier.** — /ʒə l‿e vi.zi.te jɛʁ/  
> *I visited this apartment. → I visited it yesterday.*

> **Elle a téléphoné au propriétaire. → Elle lui a téléphoné ce matin.** — /ɛl lɥi a te.le.fɔ.ne sə ma.tɛ̃/  
> *She called the landlord. → She called him/her this morning.*

In negative compound sentences:
> **Je ne l'ai pas visité.** — /ʒə nə l‿e pa vi.zi.te/ — *I did not visit it.*  
> **Elle ne lui a pas téléphoné.** — /ɛl nə lɥi a pa te.le.fɔ.ne/ — *She did not call him/her.*

#### 3. With Two Verbs (Modal and Semi-Auxiliary Structures)

When a sentence contains a conjugated verb followed by an infinitive (with verbs like **aller**, **vouloir**, **pouvoir**, **devoir**, or **aimer**), the pronoun sits **immediately before the infinitive verb that it semantically completes**:

```text
[Subject] + [Conjugated Verb] + [PRONOUN] + [Infinitive]
```

> **Je vais louer cet appartement. → Je vais le louer.** — /ʒə vɛ lə lwe/  
> *I am going to rent this apartment. → I am going to rent it.*

> **Pouvez-vous donner les clés à Paul ? → Pouvez-vous lui donner les clés ?** — /pu.ve vu lɥi dɔ.ne le kle/  
> *Can you give the keys to Paul? → Can you give him the keys?*

> **Je ne peux pas le contacter aujourd'hui.** — /ʒə nə pø pa lə kɔ̃.tak.te o.ʒuʁ.dɥi/  
> *I cannot contact him today.*

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--tip" markdown="1">

#### Québec vs France

In spoken Canadian French, object pronouns are used with great frequency to keep communication fast and natural. However, in casual informal conversations in Québec, you will occasionally hear speakers add emphasis using disjunctive tonic pronouns at the end of the sentence:
> *« Je lui ai parlé, à lui. »* or *« Je l'aime bien, elle. »*

On the TCF Canada exam, stick to standard placement without duplicate tonic pronouns:
- Written exam: *« Je lui ai envoyé tous les documents demandés. »*
- Oral exam: *« Quand j'ai rencontré le gérant, je lui ai posé plusieurs questions sur le bail. »*

Examiners award high marks under *Correction morphosyntaxique* when candidates correctly place object pronouns before infinitives and auxiliaries!

</div>

#### Exam tip: Speaking Task 2 Roleplay Strategy

On **TCF Speaking Task 2 (formal inquiry roleplay, 3.5 minutes)**, you must ask the examiner 7 to 10 spontaneous questions about a scenario (e.g. renting an apartment, booking a hotel, or inquiring about a gym).

Candidates who repeat the full noun in every single question sound like elementary learners:
- *Weak*: *« Où est l'appartement ? Est-ce que l'appartement a un balcon ? Quand est-ce que je peux visiter l'appartement ? »*
- **Strong (Level B1/B2)**: *« Où se trouve cet appartement ? A-t-**il** un balcon ? Quand est-ce que je peux **le** visiter ? Est-ce que vous pouvez **me** confirmer le montant du loyer ? »*

Using **le**, **la**, and **me** transforms your speech from robotic A1 queries into sophisticated, natural French interactions!
