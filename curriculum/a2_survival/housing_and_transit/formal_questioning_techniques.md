### Introduction

In Path 00, you learned the three fundamental registers of asking questions in French: rising intonation (*Tu habites ici ?*), standard *est-ce que* (*Est-ce que tu habites ici ?*), and basic inversion (*Habites-tu ici ?*).

While rising intonation is sufficient for casual conversation among friends, advancing to **CEFR Level A2 and B1** requires mastering **formal questioning techniques** (*l'interrogation formelle et le vouvoiement*). When dealing with landlords, bank advisers, municipal officers, healthcare workers, and official language examiners, relying solely on informal intonation sounds overly casual and grammatically rudimentary.

On the **TCF Canada examination**, formal questioning is the explicit subject of an entire speaking module:
- In **Speaking (*Expression orale*) Task 2**, you are handed an authentic classified document or situation (e.g. an apartment rental, a leisure course, a volunteer position, or an equipment rental). You are granted **2 minutes of preparation time** and must conduct a **3.5-minute face-to-face roleplay interaction** with the examiner.
- During these 3.5 minutes, **you must lead the conversation by asking 7 to 10 distinct, relevant, and well-structured questions**. France Éducation international (FEI) examiners evaluate your performance on your ability to use the polite formal register (**le vouvoiement**), vary your syntactic structures, ask for clarification, and employ indirect question formulas.

In this lesson, you will master advanced subject-verb inversion (including euphonic *-t-* and complex noun inversion), learn polite indirect question openers (*Pourriez-vous me dire si...*, *J'aimerais savoir si...*), master agreement with *quel*, and build the exact question generation framework needed to ace TCF Speaking Task 2.

### Formal pronoun inversion and euphonic rules

Subject-verb inversion (*l'inversion sujet-verbe*) is the hallmark of polished, formal French. In formal written correspondence and professional oral interactions, inversion is the preferred method for seeking information.

#### 1. Simple Pronoun Inversion

To form a simple inverted question, swap the conjugated verb and the subject pronoun, joining them with a **mandatory hyphen**:

```text
[Conjugated Verb] - [Subject Pronoun] + [Complement] ?
```

> **Avez-vous un logement disponible ?** — /a.ve vu œ̃ lɔʒ.mɑ̃ dis.pɔ.nibl/  
> *Do you have a dwelling available?*

> **Quand pouvez-vous faire la visite ?** — /kɑ̃ pu.ve vu fɛʁ la vi.zit/  
> *When can you do the viewing?*

> **Où se trouve l'appartement ?** — /u sə tʁuv l‿a.paʁ.tə.mɑ̃/  
> *Where is the apartment located?*

In compound tenses like the passé composé, you invert **the auxiliary verb and the pronoun only**; the past participle follows:
> **Avez-vous reçu mon dossier de candidature ?** — /a.ve vu ʁə.sy mɔ̃ dɔ.sje də kɑ̃.di.da.tyʁ/  
> *Have you received my application file?*

#### 2. The Euphonic -t- Insertion Rule

In spoken French, two consecutive vowel sounds clashing across an inversion boundary can sound harsh and awkward (a phonetic phenomenon known as *hiatus*).

When a third-person verb (*il*, *elle*, *on*) ends in a vowel (such as *-e* or *-a*), French inserts a hyphenated **-t-** between the verb and the pronoun:

```text
[Verb ending in vowel] + -t- + [il / elle / on] ?
```

| Normal Statement | Vowel Clash Avoided | Correct Inverted Form with Euphonic -t- | IPA Pronunciation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Il habite à Laval.** | *Habite-il ?* (Incorrect) | **Habite-t-il à Laval ?** | /a.bit.t‿il a la.val/ |
| **Elle a un balcon.** | *A-elle ?* (Incorrect) | **A-t-elle un balcon ?** | /a.t‿ɛl œ̃ bal.kɔ̃/ |
| **Le bus arrive.** | *Arrive-il ?* (Incorrect) | **Arrive-t-il bientôt ?** | /a.ʁiv.t‿il bjɛ̃.to/ |
| **On peut fumer.** | *Peut-on ?* (Already ends in -t) | **Peut-on fumer sur le balcon ?** | /pø.t‿ɔ̃ fy.me syʁ lə bal.kɔ̃/ |

Notice that if the verb already ends in **-t** or **-d** (*peut-on*, *attend-il*), no extra *-t-* is added; the existing consonant provides the liaison sound!

### Complex noun inversion in formal inquiries

What happens when the subject of your question is a **noun** (like *le loyer, l'appartement, le propriétaire*) rather than a pronoun?

In standard English, you say: *"Does the rent include electricity?"*  
In formal French, you cannot simply swap the noun and the verb (you cannot say *"Comprend le loyer... ?"*). Instead, French uses **Complex Noun Inversion** (*l'inversion complexe*):

```text
[Noun Subject] + [Conjugated Verb] - [Pronoun echoing the Noun] + [Complement] ?
```

The noun remains at the beginning of the clause, and a matching pronoun (*il, elle, ils, elles*) is attached to the verb with a hyphen:

> **Le loyer comprend-il le chauffage ?** — /lə lwa.je kɔ̃.pʁɑ̃.t‿il lə ʃo.faʒ/  
> *Does the rent include heating?* (Literally: *The rent, includes it heating?*)

> **Les animaux sont-ils acceptés dans l'immeuble ?** — /le.z‿a.ni.mo sɔ̃.t‿il.z‿ak.sɛp.te dɑ̃ l‿i.mœbl/  
> *Are pets accepted in the building?*

> **La station de métro est-elle proche du logement ?** — /la sta.sjɔ̃ də me.tʁo ɛ.t‿ɛl pʁɔʃ dy lɔʒ.mɑ̃/  
> *Is the subway station close to the dwelling?*

> **Le propriétaire a-t-il fixé une date pour la signature ?** — /lə pʁɔ.pʁi.je.tɛʁ a.t‿il fik.se yn dat puʁ la si.ɲa.tyʁ/  
> *Has the landlord set a date for the signing?*

Using complex noun inversion in formal written letters or during TCF Speaking Task 2 immediately demonstrates Upper Intermediate (B2) grammatical competence to the examiner!

### Polite indirect question openers

In high-stakes customer service, administrative inquiries, or professional housing transactions, firing direct questions one after another can sound abrasive or aggressive.

To sound polite, diplomatic, and sophisticated, native Francophones embed their questions inside **indirect question openers** (*les formules de politesse et questions indirectes*).

#### 1. Conditional Openers with "Si" (Yes/No Questions)

When asking whether something is true or possible, use an introductory conditional phrase followed by **si** (*if / whether*). Notice that inside an indirect question, **word order returns to standard subject-verb order (no inversion!)**:

```text
[Polite Opener] + si / s' + [Subject] + [Verb] ...
```

| Polite Opener | IPA Pronunciation | English Meaning | Example with *si* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pourriez-vous me dire si...** | /pu.ʁje vu mə diʁ si/ | Could you tell me if... | *Pourriez-vous me dire si le logement est libre ?* |
| **J'aimerais savoir si...** | /ʒ‿ɛm.ʁɛ sa.vwaʁ si/ | I would like to know if... | *J'aimerais savoir si l'eau chaude est comprise.* |
| **Je voudrais savoir si...** | /ʒə vu.dʁɛ sa.vwaʁ si/ | I would like to know if... | *Je voudrais savoir si un stationnement est inclus.* |
| **Savez-vous si...** | /sa.ve vu si/ | Do you know if... | *Savez-vous si les animaux sont autorisés ?* |
| **Serait-il possible de...** | /sə.ʁɛ.t‿il pɔ.sibl də/ | Would it be possible to... | *Serait-il possible de visiter ce samedi ?* |

#### 2. Openers with Interrogative Words

When inquiring about time, cost, location, or reasons, attach the interrogative word directly to the opener:

> **Pourriez-vous m'indiquer quand le bail commence ?** — /pu.ʁje vu m‿ɛ̃.di.ke kɑ̃ lə baj kɔ.mɑ̃s/  
> *Could you indicate to me when the lease begins?*

> **J'aimerais savoir combien coûte le loyer mensuel.** — /ʒ‿ɛm.ʁɛ sa.vwaʁ kɔ̃.bjɛ̃ kut lə lwa.je mɑ̃.sɥɛl/  
> *I would like to know how much the monthly rent costs.*

> **Pouvez-vous m'expliquer où se trouve la buanderie ?** — /pu.ve vu m‿ɛks.pli.ke u sə tʁuv la bɥɑ̃.dʁi/  
> *Can you explain to me where the laundry room is located?*

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--critical" markdown="1">

#### Critical Syntax Rule: No Inversion in Indirect Questions!

Never combine an indirect question opener with an inversion:
- **Incorrect**: *« Pourriez-vous me dire où est l'appartement ? »* (Direct order inversion error)
- **Correct**: *« Pourriez-vous me dire où l'appartement se trouve ? »* or *« ... où se trouve l'appartement ? »*
- **Incorrect**: *« J'aimerais savoir est-ce que le loyer est cher. »* (Never use *est-ce que* after *savoir*!)
- **Correct**: *« J'aimerais savoir si le loyer est élevé. »*

</div>

### Agreement with the interrogative adjective quel

When asking *"which"* or *"what"*, French uses the interrogative adjective **quel**. Because it is an adjective, it **must agree in gender and number with the noun it qualifies**:

| Form | Gender & Number | IPA | Example Question | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Quel** | Masc. Sing. | /kɛl/ | **Quel est le montant du loyer ?** | What is the amount of the rent? |
| **Quelle** | Fem. Sing. | /kɛl/ | **Quelle est la date de disponibilité ?** | What is the availability date? |
| **Quels** | Masc. Plur. | /kɛl/ | **Quels sont les électroménagers inclus ?**| What appliances are included? |
| **Quelles** | Fem. Plur. | /kɛl/ | **Quelles sont les conditions du bail ?** | What are the lease conditions? |

Notice that phonetically, **quel, quelle, quels,** and **quelles** all sound 100% identical: **/kɛl/**. In written French, however, matching the spelling to the gender and number of the noun is strictly evaluated under the *Correction grammaticale* criterion!

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--tip" markdown="1">

#### Québec vs France: Le Vouvoiement

In Québec culture, informal address (*le tutoiement*) is adopted much earlier in casual social environments (among colleagues, gym staff, or retail clerks) than in France.

However, in any **administrative, legal, or housing transaction**, and especially during the **TCF Canada examination**, **le vouvoiement is strictly mandatory**:
- Always address the landlord, agent, or examiner with **vous**: *« Pourriez-vous me préciser... »*, *« Êtes-vous disponible... »*.
- Never slip into *tu* during Speaking Task 2, even if the examiner maintains a warm and friendly demeanor. Treating the examiner with professional *vous* is a fundamental grading constraint.

</div>

#### Exam tip: The 7-Question Generation Grid for Speaking Task 2

During your **2 minutes of preparation time** for TCF Speaking Task 2, you are given a blank sheet of scrap paper. Do not write full sentences (you will run out of time!).

Instead, sketch this rapid **6-Point Question Generation Grid**:
1. **Lieu / Accès**: *Où se trouve... ? / Y a-t-il un métro à proximité ?*
2. **Prix / Modalités**: *Quel est le prix ? / Quels sont les frais supplémentaires ?*
3. **Disponibilité / Horaires**: *À quelle date est-ce libre ? / Quels sont les horaires ?*
4. **Équipements / Services**: *L'immeuble dispose-t-il d'un ascenseur / d'un stationnement ?*
5. **Règles / Conditions**: *Les animaux sont-ils acceptés ? / Est-ce non-fumeur ?*
6. **Rendez-vous / Suite**: *Quand serait-il possible de se rencontrer pour visiter ?*

This grid guarantees that you will never run out of questions during the 3.5-minute interaction!
