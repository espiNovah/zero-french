### Introduction

Finding and securing a place to live is one of the very first real-world milestones every newcomer to Canada or France experiences. Whether browsing classified ads online, deciphering rental listings, negotiating lease terms with a landlord, or inspecting amenities, housing is an indispensable daily survival skill.

On the **TCF Canada examination**, housing listings and real estate transactions are prominent across all four evaluation modules:
- In **Reading (*Compréhension écrite*)**, short everyday documents such as classified ads (*petites annonces immobilières*) are typical of the early, easier items (items get progressively harder as the test goes on). The exam evaluates your ability to quickly scan abbreviations, check rental costs, determine whether utilities are included, and verify conditions such as pet policies or elevator access.
- In **Listening (*Compréhension orale*)**, audio dialogues simulate tenant-landlord phone inquiries, inquiries about apartment viewings, or roommate interviews.
- In **Speaking (*Expression orale*) Task 2**, you are routinely handed a prompt asking you to roleplay inquiring about an apartment for rent (*« Vous téléphonez au propriétaire d'un appartement à louer pour poser des questions sur le loyer, les charges, les pièces et les conditions »*). You lead the exchange by asking the examiner fluent, spontaneous questions (aiming for 7 to 10 is a useful practice target, not an official rule).

In this lesson, you will master the vocabulary of rooms, amenities, and furnishings, decode confusing real estate abbreviations, understand the unique **Québec 1½ to 5½ apartment numbering system**, learn critical Canadian tenancy laws, and practice scanning authentic rental ads under timed exam conditions.

### Lesson overview

This section contains a general overview of topics that you will learn in this lesson.

- The layout and vocabulary of home interiors: rooms, spaces, and essential domestic fixtures.

- Essential tenancy and lease vocabulary (*le loyer, le bail, le propriétaire, le locataire, les charges*).

### The two systems of apartment sizing: Europe vs Canada

One of the greatest points of confusion for newcomers is how apartments are counted. France and Québec use entirely different sizing methodologies.

#### 1. The French System: Studios, T1, T2, and T3

In France and Europe, apartments are classified by the letter **T** (for *Type*) or **F** (for *Fonction*), followed by the number of **habitable living rooms** (living room + bedrooms). Bathrooms and kitchens are never counted in this number:

- **Studio**: One single open room serving as bedroom and living room, with a kitchenette and separate bathroom.
- **T1 / F1**: One living room/bedroom, plus a separate kitchen and bathroom.
- **T2 / F2**: Two rooms = **1 bedroom** + 1 living room (plus kitchen and bathroom).
- **T3 / F3**: Three rooms = **2 bedrooms** + 1 living room (plus kitchen and bathroom).
- **T4 / F4**: Four rooms = **3 bedrooms** + 1 living room.

#### 2. The Canadian Québec System: The Half-Room System (1½ to 5½)

In Québec and Francophone Canada, rental listings universally use the **half-room system** (**la demie**). The half (½) represents the **bathroom**:

- **Un 1½** (/œ̃ œ̃.n‿e dmi/): A studio apartment (one main room + one bathroom).
- **Un 2½** (/œ̃ dø.z‿e dmi/): One living/sleeping area, a separate kitchen, plus one bathroom.
- **Un 3½** (/œ̃ tʁwa.z‿e dmi/): **One bedroom**, one living room, one separate kitchen, plus one bathroom. (Equivalent to a French *T2* or North American "one-bedroom").
- **Un 4½** (/œ̃ katʁ.e dmi/): **Two bedrooms**, one living room, one kitchen, plus one bathroom. (Equivalent to a French *T3* or North American "two-bedroom").
- **Un 5½** (/œ̃ sɛ̃k.e dmi/): **Three bedrooms**, one living room, one kitchen, plus one bathroom.

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--critical" markdown="1">

#### Critical Comparison: French T3 vs Québec 3½

Never mix up these two notations on the TCF Canada exam:
- In France, a **T3** has **two bedrooms** (3 main rooms minus 1 living room = 2 bedrooms).
- In Québec, a **3½** has only **one bedroom** (1 living room + 1 kitchen + 1 bedroom = 3 rooms, plus ½ for the bathroom)!

If a reading question asks: *« Combien de chambres compte ce logement québécois de 4½ ? »*, the correct answer is **deux chambres**!

</div>

### Decoding classified ads and real estate abbreviations

Real estate listings in newspapers and online classified sites (such as Kijiji, Marketplace, or SeLoger) use dense abbreviations to save space:

| Abbreviation | Full French Form | English Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **rdc / RDC** | *rez-de-chaussée* | ground floor |
| **asc.** | *ascenseur* | elevator |
| **étag.** | *étage* | floor / story (e.g. *3e étag.* = 3rd floor) |
| **meub.** | *meublé* | furnished |
| **non meub.** | *non meublé* | unfurnished |
| **sdb / SDB** | *salle de bain* | bathroom |
| **sde / SDE** | *salle d'eau* | shower room (shower without bathtub) |
| **cc / CC** | *charges comprises* | utilities / building fees included in rent |
| **hc / HC** | *hors charges* | rent without utilities / extra costs |
| **électros** **(QC)** | *électroménagers inclus* | refrigerator and stove included |
| **stat.** **(QC)** | *stationnement* | parking space |

#### Sample Classified Ad Analysis

> **Montréal (Plateau) — 4½ rénové, 2e étag., balcon, non meub., électros inclus. Chauffé / éclairé. 1 450 $/mois. Libre 1er juillet. Animaux non admis. Tél : 514-555-0144.**

Let us deconstruct this authentic listing:
- **4½ rénové**: 2 bedrooms, living room, kitchen, bathroom, recently renovated.
- **2e étag., balcon**: Located on the second floor, with an outdoor balcony.
- **non meub., électros inclus**: Unfurnished, but the refrigerator and stove are supplied by the landlord.
- **Chauffé / éclairé**: Heat and electricity are included in the monthly rent.
- **1 450 $/mois**: Rent is $1,450 CAD per month.
- **Libre 1er juillet**: Available for move-in on July 1st.
- **Animaux non admis**: Pets forbidden.

### Rental terms, lease contracts, and Canadian legal realities

Navigating the rental market requires specific contractual and financial terminology:

- **Le loyer** (/lə lwa.je/) — *The rent*: The monthly financial sum paid to the owner.
- **Le bail** (/lə baj/) — *The lease / rental contract* (plural: *les baux* /le bo/).
- **Le propriétaire** (/lə pʁɔ.pʁi.je.tɛʁ/) — *The landlord / owner* (feminine: *la propriétaire*).
- **Le locataire** (/lə lɔ.ka.tɛʁ/) — *The tenant* (feminine: *la locataire*).
- **La colocation** (/la kɔ.lɔ.ka.sjɔ̃/) — *Apartment sharing / living with roommates*. (Roommate = *un colocataire* or *un coloc*).
- **Le dépôt de garantie / La caution** (/lə de.po də ɡa.ʁɑ̃.ti / la ko.sjɔ̃/) — *Security deposit*.

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--tip" markdown="1">

#### Québec vs France: Le Bail and Moving Day

In France, landlords routinely require a **dépôt de garantie** (security deposit equal to 1 or 2 months' rent) before handing over keys.

In **Québec**, under the *Civil Code of Québec* (art. 1904), **landlords are legally forbidden from demanding a security deposit or damage deposit**! (The *Tribunal administratif du logement*, TAL, is the tribunal that settles landlord–tenant disputes.) A landlord may only ask for the first month's rent in advance upon signing the lease.

Furthermore, in Québec culture, July 1st is popularly called **le jour du déménagement** (Moving Day). A large share of residential leases in Québec begin on July 1st and end on June 30th of the following year. On July 1st across Montreal and Quebec City, hundreds of thousands of residents change apartments simultaneously.

When you encounter Canadian reading or listening texts mentioning *« chercher un logement pour le premier juillet »*, this is the cultural reality being referenced!

</div>

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--warning" markdown="1">

#### Exam tip: TCF Reading Classified Ad Traps

On the early **Compréhension écrite** items, classified ads test your precision scanning:

1. **Beware of Negations and Exclusions**: Ads often specify conditions at the very end using abbreviations or terse formulas:
   - *« Animaux refusés »* or *« Chiens interdits »* = No pets allowed.
   - *« Non-fumeur seulement »* = Smoking strictly forbidden.
   - *« Pas d'ascenseur »* = Walk-up building only (candidates with limited mobility trap).
2. **Charges Comprises (CC) vs Hors Charges (HC)**: Questions often ask how much total money the tenant will pay each month:
   - If rent is *800 € CC*, the tenant pays 800 € total.
   - If rent is *800 € HC + 50 € de charges*, the total monthly payment is 850 €!
3. **Availability Date (*Disponibilité*)**: Words like *libre immédiatement*, *disponible à compter du 1er mai*, or *bail d'un an minimum* frequently contain the exact answer to timeline questions.

Train your eyes to scan directly for digits, abbreviations, and exclusionary symbols before reading the entire paragraph!

</div>
