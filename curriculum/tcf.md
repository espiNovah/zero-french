### TCF Canada

The **Test de connaissance du français pour le Canada** (TCF Canada) is the official French proficiency test accepted by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) for economic immigration and citizenship applications.

This page summarises the exam format, scoring and the NCLC conversion you need to understand before you start studying. Lessons reference this page when they mention the exam.

**Last checked:** 2026-10-29 against [France Éducation international](https://www.france-education-international.fr/test/tcf-canada) and [IRCC equivalency charts](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-requirements/language-testing.html). If this date is more than six months old, verify the official sources before booking your test.

### The four parts

The TCF Canada has **four mandatory parts**. Total test time is approximately **2 hours 47 minutes**.

| Part | Format | Items | Time | Score range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Listening** (*Compréhension orale*) | 39 multiple-choice questions (4 choices, 1 correct). Audio played once. Progressive difficulty. | 39 questions | 35 min | 0–699 |
| **Reading** (*Compréhension écrite*) | 39 multiple-choice questions (4 choices, 1 correct). Progressive difficulty from simple notices to complex articles. | 39 questions | 60 min | 0–699 |
| **Writing** (*Expression écrite*) | 3 written tasks of increasing complexity. | 3 tasks | 60 min | 0–20 |
| **Speaking** (*Expression orale*) | 3 tasks, face-to-face with an examiner. Recorded. | 3 tasks | 12 min | 0–20 |

### Writing tasks in detail

| Task | What you write | Word count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Task 1** | A message to describe, narrate or explain something, addressed to a specified recipient. | 60–120 words |
| **Task 2** | An article, letter or note giving an account of an experience or a story, with opinions or arguments. | 120–150 words |
| **Task 3** | A text comparing two points of view on a social issue (from two provided documents), plus your own opinion. | 120–180 words |

### Speaking tasks in detail

| Task | What you do | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Task 1 — Guided interview** (*Entretien dirigé*) | Talk about yourself, your background, family and work. No preparation. | 2 min |
| **Task 2 — Role-play** (*Exercice en interaction*) | Obtain information in a daily-life situation (e.g. booking a service). A prompt gives the roles. | 5 min 30 (incl. 2 min prep) |
| **Task 3 — Expressing a point of view** (*Expression d'un point de vue*) | Respond spontaneously to a question chosen by the examiner: present and defend your opinion. | 4 min 30, no prep |

### Scoring and CEFR levels

Listening and reading are scored on a 0–699 scale. Writing and speaking are scored on a 0–20 scale. Each part is graded independently — there is no overall combined score.

| CEFR level | Listening (0–699) | Reading (0–699) | Writing (0–20) | Speaking (0–20) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | 100–199 | 100–199 | 1 | 1 |
| A2 | 200–299 | 200–299 | 2–5 | 2–5 |
| B1 | 300–399 | 300–399 | 6–9 | 6–9 |
| B2 | 400–499 | 400–499 | 10–13 | 10–13 |
| C1 | 500–599 | 500–599 | 14–17 | 14–17 |
| C2 | 600–699 | 600–699 | 18–20 | 18–20 |

### NCLC conversion (IRCC)

IRCC converts your TCF Canada scores to **Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadiens** (NCLC) levels. The table below shows the minimum TCF score needed for each NCLC level.

| NCLC | Listening | Reading | Writing | Speaking |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **10+** | 549–699 | 549–699 | 16–20 | 16–20 |
| **9** | 523–548 | 524–548 | 14–15 | 14–15 |
| **8** | 503–522 | 499–523 | 12–13 | 12–13 |
| **7** | 458–502 | 453–498 | 10–11 | 10–11 |
| **6** | 398–457 | 406–452 | 7–9 | 7–9 |
| **5** | 369–397 | 375–405 | 6 | 6 |
| **4** | 331–368 | 342–374 | 4–5 | 4–5 |

**For Express Entry:** NCLC 7 in all four skills gives maximum CRS points for French as a first official language. That means you need at least:

- Listening: **458**
- Reading: **453**
- Writing: **10**
- Speaking: **10**

This curriculum aims for scores comfortably above these thresholds so that exam-day nerves don't cost you points.

### Validity and retakes

- Results are **valid for 2 years** from the date of issue.
- You can retake the TCF Canada as often as you like, but you must wait **at least 20 calendar days** between sittings.
- There is **no penalty for wrong answers** on the multiple-choice sections — always answer every question.

### Key strategy points

- Questions in listening and reading go from easy to hard. Don't spend too long on difficult questions — answer and move on.
- In writing, respect the word counts strictly. Going under the minimum or skipping a task can result in "below A1."
- In speaking, use *vous* with the examiner, begin with *Bonjour* and end with *Au revoir*. Structure your answers: state your point, give reasons, conclude.
- Practice under timed conditions regularly. The curriculum's projects are designed for this.
