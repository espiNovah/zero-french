### Introduction

Congratulations. You have arrived at the final capstone project of **Path 05: B2+ Safety Margin (C1 Foundations)**—and the 110th, concluding milestone of the entire **Zéro French** curriculum.

Every lesson across this curriculum has been engineered with a single strategic objective: **to secure certified Canadian Francophone language results (NCLC 7+) for permanent residence under Express Entry and the Provincial Nominee Programs (PNP).** (The separate *Mobilité francophone* stream is a temporary work-permit pathway that requires NCLC 5, not a permanent-residence program.)

With **NCLC 7 or higher in all four French skills**, Express Entry grants:
- **50 additional Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points** if your English is at CLB 5 or higher, or **25 additional CRS points** if your English is at CLB 4 or lower. The two amounts don't stack: the maximum French bonus is 50.
- Eligibility for **category-based selection draws for French-language proficiency**.

NCLC 7 is a **threshold**, not a ceiling: CRS language points keep rising above it, so every extra level counts.

However, many candidates prepare narrowly for a bare-minimum score—only to fall just short on exam day due to test-center noise, an unexpected distractor in reading, an unexpected examiner question in speaking, or keyboard fatigue in writing.

This final milestone mock exam reduces that risk through the **C1 Safety Margin Protocol**.

By calibrating your skills to **NCLC 8+** (IRCC equivalency table for TCF Canada):
- **Compréhension orale**: Target $\ge$ **503 / 699 pts** (NCLC 8)
- **Compréhension écrite**: Target $\ge$ **499 / 699 pts** (NCLC 8)
- **Expression écrite**: Target $\ge$ **12 / 20 pts** (NCLC 8)
- **Expression orale**: Target $\ge$ **12 / 20 pts** (NCLC 8)

If adverse test-day conditions, circadian fatigue, or test-center anxiety pull your performance down, a margin of a full NCLC level gives you room to **stay at NCLC 7 or above**. No margin is a guarantee, but it greatly improves your odds.

### Workstation configuration and exam day simulation rules

To ensure that your diagnostic score is uninflated, enforce official test center conditions (*TCF pour le Canada sur ordinateur*):

1. **Continuous 3-Hour Time Block**: Reserve a continuous block of **2 hours and 47 minutes**:
   - *Part 1: Compréhension orale* — 35 minutes (39 items, audio played once)
   - *Part 2: Compréhension écrite* — 60 minutes (39 items, front-loaded pacing)
   - *Optional break* — a short pause for restroom and hydration (not counted in the 2 h 47; break arrangements on test day vary, so check your *convocation*)
   - *Part 3: Expression écrite* — 60 minutes (3 typed tasks, zero autocorrect)
   - *Part 4: Expression orale* — 12 minutes (3 recorded tasks)
2. **Physical Workspace Constraints**:
   - Close all browser tabs, dictionaries, grammar references, and social media.
   - Use a desktop or laptop with a physical keyboard. Disable all spelling and grammar extensions (Grammarly, LanguageTool, Antidote).
   - Use standard over-ear or in-ear headphones for audio sections.
   - Have an audio recording app ready to record Part 4 without stopping.
3. **No Pauses or Re-takes**: Once an audio track begins, you cannot pause, rewind, or replay it: in the TCF, each recording is played only once. For on-screen navigation details, check the FEI candidate guide.

#### Part 1: Compréhension orale (35 Minutes — 39 Questions)
Work through a complete 39-question listening mock built from practice material, for example from [TV5Monde — Entraînement au TCF](https://apprendre.tv5monde.com/fr) or [France Éducation international](https://www.france-education-international.fr/).
- Before each audio, read the question and options so you know what to listen for.
- Apply the **Five-Second Drop Rule**: if you hesitate between two options, commit to your best choice and move on.
- Expect the later items to be harder: faster speech, multi-speaker exchanges, implicit irony, and subtle stance markers.

---

#### Part 2: Compréhension écrite (60 Minutes — 39 Questions)
Complete a 39-question reading practice sitting with a **front-loaded pacing plan**:
- **Early items (easier)**: move briskly; don't over-check short, simple texts.
- **Later items (harder)**: bank the time you saved for the longer, denser texts at the end.
- There is no official item-number→level map: items simply get progressively harder.
- Beware of absolute lexical distractors (*toujours, uniquement, sans exception*) and false friends (*éventuellement, opportunité*).

#### Part 3: Expression écrite Simulation (60 Minutes — 3 Tasks)
Open a blank plain-text editor. Type all three tasks continuously within your 60-minute window without consulting any external aids:

##### Task 1: Message d'information / de demande (Budget: 12 minutes — 60 to 120 words)
> **Sujet** : Vous êtes membre d'une association citoyenne de votre quartier. Vous écrivez un courriel à la direction des parcs municipaux pour signaler la dégradation d'un espace vert communautaire et proposer deux initiatives concrètes de réaménagement collectif.

##### Task 2: Courrier d'opinion / Témoignage (Budget: 20 minutes — 120 to 150 words)
> **Sujet** : Dans le journal local, un éditorialiste affirme que les municipalités devraient interdire l'usage des trottinettes électriques en libre-service car elles constituent un danger pour les piétons. Vous écrivez au courrier des lecteurs pour exprimer votre désaccord nuancé. Vous utilisez le **conditionnel passé** pour évoquer ce que la ville aurait dû faire pour encadrer ce service plutôt que de l'interdire.

##### Task 3: Synthèse et prise de position dialectique (Budget: 28 minutes — 120 to 180 words)
> **Documents sources** :  
> *Document 1 : Le Devoir* — La semaine de quatre jours sans baisse de salaire améliore la productivité, préserve la santé mentale des salariés et réduit l'empreinte écologique liée aux déplacements quotidiens.  
> *Document 2 : La Presse* — Réduire le temps de travail hebdomadaire aggraverait la pénurie criante de main-d'œuvre dans les services essentiels (santé, éducation, transport) et asphyxierait financièrement les petites entreprises.  
> **Consigne** : Résumez objectivement les deux opinions sans copier de phrases mot à mot (environ 40–60 mots), puis défendez votre point de vue personnel nuancé en intégrant au moins un **subjonctif passé** et une **métaphore civique** (environ 80–120 mots). (Total : 120–180 mots).

#### Part 4: Expression orale Simulation (12 Minutes — 3 Recorded Tasks)
Launch your audio recording device. Deliver all three tasks without stopping the recording:

##### Task 1: Entretien dirigé sans préparation (2 minutes)
- Present yourself formally: professional trajectory, motivations for immigrating to Canada, and intellectual passions.
- Maintain formal syntactic structures: passive voice, causal gerunds (*« Ayant exercé pendant cinq ans en tant qu'ingénieur... »*), and balanced rhythmic groups.

##### Task 2: Exercice en interaction (2 minutes de préparation + 3.5 minutes)
- Scenario: You call a municipal co-working cooperative to negotiate the hosting of a community tech workshop.
- Ask 6 diverse syntactic questions (inversion, *est-ce que*, indirect interrogatives).
- Overcome at least two logistical objections regarding insurance, noise, and scheduling.

##### Task 3: Expression d'un point de vue sans préparation (4.5 minutes)
- **Sujet** : *« Pour lutter contre la sédentarité et l'obésité infantile, les pouvoirs publics devraient imposer une taxe spéciale sur les consoles de jeux vidéo et les abonnements aux plateformes de diffusion en continu. »*
- Structure: Present and defend your point of view for the full 4 min 30 (*Ancrage conceptuel*, *Déconstruction critique*, *Dépassement constructif*). The real examiner **may intervene** with a question or objection—it is not an adversarial debate. If a practice partner interjects, answer calmly using the **Concession-Pivot-Counterattack Triad** with flawless syllable-timed prosody.

> **L'éditorial plaidant pour l'interdiction pure et simple des trottinettes électriques en libre-service témoigne d'une réaction disproportionnée face à une transition des mobilités urbaines.**

> **Les deux documents confrontent les perspectives entourant l'instauration de la semaine de quatre jours. Le premier texte souligne ses retombées bénéfiques sur le bien-être des salariés, la productivité et la transition écologique. À l'inverse, le second document alerte sur le péril d'un tel aménagement pour les PME et sur l'aggravation inévitable de la pénurie de main-d'œuvre dans les secteurs névralgiques.**

Dans les services hospitaliers et les transports collectifs, une telle mesure asphyxierait les effectifs restants.**  
> **Dès lors, il importe de faire la part des choses : plutôt qu'une contrainte légale généralisée, l'État devrait encourager des accords de branche concertés. Cette flexibilité sectorielle permettrait de moderniser les conditions de travail sans compromettre la viabilité économique de nos services essentiels.**

### Go/No-Go final decision gate before booking the official TCF sitting

Before paying your test fees (they vary by centre) and scheduling your sitting at an accredited test center, your mock performance must clear the **Seven-Point Go/No-Go Gate**:

1. [ ] **Receptive Score Gate**: Did you reach **NCLC 8+ in both Listening (≥ 503) and Reading (≥ 499)** on a scored mock (securing the NCLC 8 safety buffer)?
2. [ ] **Writing Word-Count Discipline**: Did every written task respect its length range (Task 1: 60–120w, Task 2: 120–150w, Task 3: 120–180w)?
3. [ ] **Syntactic Sophistication**: Did your writing and speaking include at least one conditionnel passé, one subjonctif passé, and two C1 rhetorical articulators?
4. [ ] **Authentic Idiomatic Control**: Did you incorporate consecrated civic metaphors (*faire la part des choses*, *mettre la charrue avant les bœufs*, *de la poudre aux yeux*) without popular slang?
5. [ ] **Zero-Autocorrect Certification**: Were all written tasks produced with zero spellcheck, grammar plugins, or digital aids?
6. [ ] **Syllable-Timed Prosody**: Did your speaking recording exhibit regular syllable duration, rhythmic group segmentation, and zero English word-stress reduction?
7. [ ] **Cognitive Resilience**: Did you recover from exam hesitations in under 2 seconds without panicking?

> **DECISION RULE**:  
> - **If all 7 criteria are checked**: **GO**. Book your official TCF Canada sitting. You have a realistic safety margin above NCLC 7 (no result is ever guaranteed).  
> - **If 1 or 2 criteria fail**: Spend 5 days reviewing the specific targeted corrective sprint in Path 04 before testing again.
