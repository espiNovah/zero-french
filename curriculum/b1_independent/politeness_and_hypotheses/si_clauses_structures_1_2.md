### Introduction

A hallmark of mature human cognition is the ability to project beyond immediate physical realities: imagining alternative possibilities, weighing potential consequences, planning contingencies, and reflecting on what could happen under differing circumstances. In linguistic terms, this capacity is expressed through **hypothetical sentences with "si"** (*les propositions hypothétiques avec "si"*), commonly referred to in grammar as **conditional clauses** or **si-clauses**.

At CEFR Level B1, mastering hypothetical structures is not merely an academic exercise; it is the cornerstone of reasoned decision-making, professional negotiation, and persuasive argument. Whether you are analyzing economic alternatives during a business meeting (*« Si nous réduisons nos coûts, nous pourrons embaucher »*) or contemplating life milestones in Canada (*« Si j'obtenais ma résidence permanente, je m'installerais à Québec »*), hypothetical clauses allow you to articulate complex logic with elegance and precision.

On the **TCF Canada examination**, *si-clauses* are among the highest-yield grammatical structures for scoring at **Level B1 and B2 (NCLC 6–8)**:
- In **Speaking (*Expression orale*) Task 3 (unscripted opinion debate, 4.5 minutes)**, formulating balanced arguments requires exploring cause-and-effect projections: *« Si les gouvernements amélioraient le réseau ferroviaire, les citoyens utiliseraient moins leur véhicule personnel »*.
- In **Writing (*Expression écrite*) Task 2 & Task 3**, examiners evaluate your capacity to present hypothetical proposals and counter-arguments using varied syntactic subordinate clauses.
- In **Listening and Reading Comprehension**, understanding whether an event is a genuine factual certainty or merely a conditional hypothesis prevents critical comprehension errors.

### The anatomy of French hypothetical structures with si

A French hypothetical sentence consists of two distinct components linked by a cause-and-effect relationship:

1. **La proposition subordonnée de condition (The "Si" Clause)**: Establishes the condition, contingency, or circumstance. It is introduced by the conjunction **si** (*if*).
2. **La proposition principale (The Main / Result Clause)**: Expresses the outcome, consequence, or resulting action that occurs if the condition is satisfied.

```text
[Condition Clause]                     [Result Clause]
Si vous réussissez le test, ---------> vous obtiendrez votre visa.
(Condition: If you pass the test)      (Consequence: you will obtain your visa)
```

The mood and tense used in both clauses must follow strict, mathematically predictable pairings. You cannot mix tenses arbitrarily. In this lesson, you will master the first two fundamental structures.

### Hypothesis 1: Real and probable conditions in the future

Structure 1 expresses conditions that are **real, possible, and likely to occur** in the future. If the condition in the present is fulfilled, the result in the future will logically happen.

#### 1. The standard formula: Si + Présent → Futur Simple

$$\mathbf{\text{Si}} + \mathbf{\text{Présent de l'indicatif}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Futur simple}}$$

| Condition Clause (*Si + Présent*) | Result Clause (*Futur Simple*) | IPA Pronunciation | English Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **S'il fait beau samedi,** | **nous irons au parc.** | /s‿il fɛ bo sam.di nu z‿i.ʁɔ̃ o paʁk/ | *If the weather is nice Saturday, we will go to the park.* |
| **Si vous signez ce contrat,** | **vous commencerez lundi.** | /si vu si.ɲe sə kɔ̃.tʁa vu kɔ.mɑ̃.sə.ʁe lœ̃.di/ | *If you sign this contract, you will start on Monday.* |
| **Si nous travaillons ensemble,** | **nous réussirons le projet.** | /si nu tʁa.va.jɔ̃ ɑ̃.sɑ̃bl nu ʁe.y.si.ʁɔ̃ lə pʁɔ.ʒɛ/ | *If we work together, we will succeed on the project.* |
| **Si j'ai le temps ce soir,** | **je t'enverrai le document.** | /si ʒ‿e lə tɑ̃ sə swaʁ ʒə t‿ɑ̃.vɛ.ʁe lə dɔ.ky.mɑ̃/ | *If I have time this evening, I will send you the document.* |

#### 2. Functional variants: Futur Proche and Impératif

Depending on your communicative purpose, the result clause can adopt two other verb forms:

- **Si + Présent → Futur Proche (Immediate or informal consequence)**:  
  *« Si tu arrives en retard, **le train va partir** sans toi. »*  
  (*If you arrive late, the train is going to leave without you.*)
- **Si + Présent → Impératif (Direct instruction, advice, or warning)**:  
  *« Si vous avez des questions sur votre dossier, **appelez** le centre de service. »*  
  (*If you have questions about your file, call the service center.*)  
  *« S'il commence à neiger, **ralentis** et conduis prudemment. »*  
  (*If it starts to snow, slow down and drive carefully.*)

### Hypothesis 2: Potential and contrary-to-fact present conditions

Structure 2 shifts away from tangible likelihood into the realm of **imagination, speculation, potential futures, or present situations that are contrary to current reality**.

#### 1. The standard formula: Si + Imparfait → Conditionnel Présent

$$\mathbf{\text{Si}} + \mathbf{\text{Imparfait}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Conditionnel présent}}$$

| Condition Clause (*Si + Imparfait*) | Result Clause (*Conditionnel Présent*) | IPA Pronunciation | English Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Si j'avais plus de temps libre,** | **j'apprendrais une autre langue.** | /si ʒ‿a.vɛ ply də tɑ̃ libʁ ʒ‿a.pʁɑ̃.dʁɛ yn otʁ lɑ̃ɡ/ | *If I had more free time, I would learn another language.* |
| **Si nous habitions à Montréal,** | **nous prendrions le métro.** | /si nu z‿a.bi.tjɔ̃ a mɔ̃.ʁe.al nu pʁɑ̃.dʁi.jɔ̃ lə me.tʁo/ | *If we lived in Montreal, we would take the metro.* |
| **Si tu faisais plus de sport,** | **tu te sentirais beaucoup mieux.** | /si ty fə.zɛ ply də spɔʁ ty tə sɑ̃.ti.ʁɛ bo.ku mjø/ | *If you did more sports, you would feel much better.* |
| **Si le loyer était moins cher,** | **ils loueraient cet appartement.** | /si lə lwa.jɛ e.tɛ mwɛ̃ ʃɛʁ il lwe.ʁɛ sɛt a.paʁ.tə.mɑ̃/ | *If the rent were cheaper, they would rent this apartment.* |

#### 2. Interpreting the reality of Structure 2

When you say *« Si j'avais de l'argent, j'achèterais cette maison »*, the grammatical reality is that **you do not currently have the money** in the present moment. The condition is hypothetical (*imaginaire*), and the conditional expresses what *would* happen if the current reality were different.

<div class="lesson-note lesson-note--warning" markdown="1">

**The cardinal grammatical rule: Les "si" n'aiment pas les "-rais" !**  
In French grammar, one of the most frequent errors committed by learners is placing the *conditionnel* or the *futur* directly inside the clause preceded by **si**.  
French syntax strictly prohibits this:
- **INCORRECT**: *Si j'aurais de l'argent, je voyagerais...* (Catastrophic error!).
- **INCORRECT**: *Si j'achèterai une voiture, je serai content...*
- **CORRECT**: **Si j'avais de l'argent**, je voyagerais.
- **CORRECT**: **Si j'achète une voiture**, je serai content.

Remember the classic mnemonic rhyme taught in Francophone schools:  
*« Les "si" n'aiment jamais les "-rais" ! »* (The word *si* never tolerates verbs ending in *-rais*).

</div>

### Word order comma punctuation and the elision rule for si

#### 1. Inverting the clauses and comma rules

The order of the two clauses in a hypothetical sentence is completely flexible:
1. **Condition first**: If the *si* clause opens the sentence, a **comma is mandatory** between the two clauses:  
   *« **Si vous déménagez à Québec,** vous découvrirez une ville magnifique. »*
2. **Result first**: If the main result clause opens the sentence, **no comma is used**:  
   *« Vous découvrirez une ville magnifique **si vous déménagez à Québec**. »*  
   *« J'achèterais ce vélo **si j'avais assez d'économies**. »*

#### 2. Elision rules with the conjunction si

Pay careful attention to vowel elision when **si** encounters subject pronouns:
- **Si + il** -> **S'il** (*mandatory elision*):  
  *S'il pleut demain...* (/s‿il plø də.mɛ̃/)
- **Si + ils** -> **S'ils** (*mandatory elision*):  
  *S'ils acceptent notre proposition...* (/s‿il z‿ak.sɛpt/)
- **Si + elle / elles** -> **Si elle / Si elles** (*NEVER elide!*):  
  *« Si elle vient... »* (Not: *S'elle vient* — incorrect!).
- **Si + on** -> **Si on** (in spoken French) or **Si l'on** (in formal and written French):  
  *« Si on étudie tous les jours... »* or *« Si l'on étudie tous les jours... »* (the euphonic *l'* prevents a hiatus between /i/ and /ɔ̃/).

### TCF Canada exam focus with hypothetical reasoning

Hypothetical constructions serve as an essential score lever on the TCF Canada exam:

1. **Expression orale Tâche 3 (Opinion Monologue — 4.5 minutes)**:
   - Evaluators evaluate candidates on their ability to project hypotheses when addressing questions such as:  
     *« Pensez-vous que le travail à distance devrait être généralisé pour tous les employés ? »*
   - A basic candidate answers with flat present statements: *« Le télétravail est bien, les gens économisent du temps. »*
   - A Level B1/B2 candidate deploys hypothetical balance:  
     *« **Si les entreprises accordaient** davantage de journées de télétravail, les employés **auraient** une meilleure conciliation travail-famille. Cependant, **si les équipes restent** constamment isolées à la maison, le sentiment d'appartenance collective **diminuera**. C'est pourquoi **si l'on adoptait** une formule hybride de deux jours par semaine, tout le monde **y trouverait** son compte. »*
2. **Expression écrite Tâche 2 (Formal Letter / Opinion — 120–150 words)**:
   - When writing a letter to a municipal authority or landlord proposing changes, deploy *si-clauses* to show foresight:  
     *« **Si vous installiez** des pistes cyclables sécurisées sur cette avenue, les citoyens **délaisseraient** leur automobile et la pollution **diminuerait**. »*
3. **Listening & Reading Comprehension**:
   - Pay close attention to verb moods in announcements. If an ad states: *« Si vous souscrivez avant le 31 octobre, vous bénéficierez d'un rabais de 20 % »*, recognize that the discount is contingent on meeting the October 31st deadline.
